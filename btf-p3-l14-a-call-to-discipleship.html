Part 3 of 3: Serving in Christ

A Call to Discipleship

Lesson 14 of 16  |  Section D: Living as a Disciple

Opening Question

Please begin by answering this question honestly in your own words.

Has anyone ever personally invested in helping you grow as a follower of Christ, one-on-one, over time? What difference did that make?

Key Scripture

Luke 14:33

So likewise, whoever of you does not forsake all that he has cannot be My disciple.

Read this slowly before moving on. Jesus does not soften this cost to make it easier to hear.

Core Truth

The central idea of this lesson

Christ’s commission was never merely to make converts; it was to make disciples, transformed believers capable of reproducing genuine faith in others.

A cost named plainly

Jesus deliberately distinguished discipleship from casual interest, calling for a level of commitment that would "forsake all," not a one-time decision alone.

Luke 14:26

If anyone comes to Me and does not hate his father and mother, wife and children, brothers and sisters, yes, and his own life also, he cannot be My disciple.

Make disciples, not only converts

The Great Commission’s actual instruction is to make disciples, not simply to secure decisions or count converts.

Matthew 28:19-20

Go therefore and make disciples of all the nations, baptizing them in the name of the Father and of the Son and of the Holy Spirit, teaching them to observe all things that I have commanded you.

Multiplication, not only addition

A discipleship approach that reproduces itself multiplies exponentially over time, in principle reaching far more people over years than large-scale but shallow campaigns.

Going Deeper

A mathematical illustration is worth working through slowly: one person discipling another, who then disciples another, doubling consistently. Over roughly twelve to fifteen years of consistent doubling, the numbers surpass what mass crusades typically reach in the same period, not because reproduction is faster in any single instance, but because it compounds.

Christian and disciple: one commitment

"Christian" and "disciple" are meant to describe the same level of commitment; the terms were used interchangeably in the early church.

Acts 11:26

And when he had found him, he brought him to Antioch. So it was that for a whole year they assembled with the church and taught a great many people. And the disciples were first called Christians in Antioch.

Note

Discipleship requires patient, personal investment, much like raising a child, not a single event but sustained responsibility over time.

Question My Answer Group Discussion Notes
Luke 14:33 uses strong language: "forsake all." What has following Christ actually cost you so far?
What is the practical difference between making a convert and making a disciple, as this lesson defines it?
Who has personally discipled you, even informally? What did that investment look like?
Is there someone you could begin intentionally discipling, even in a small way? What would the first step be?

These questions are designed for open conversation at any level of experience. There are no trick questions and no single correct answer.

  1. Why do you think churches often default to measuring success by attendance or decisions rather than by disciples who go on to disciple others?
  2. Share what the "forsake all" language in Luke 14:33 means to you practically, not just theoretically.
  3. How does the multiplication illustration change your view of what one faithful discipling relationship could accomplish over a lifetime?
  4. What makes discipleship harder than simply inviting someone to a church event or crusade?

Discipleship is personal and sustained, not a single event. Take one real step toward investing in someone this week.

Context How I Apply This
Personal life Think of one person you could begin discipling, even informally, and take a first step toward that relationship this week.
Church community Reflect on Luke 14:33 and identify what "forsaking all" currently means for you in practice, not just in theory.
Difficult seasons If you already invite people to church events, add one act of personal, sustained investment in someone’s spiritual growth this week.
Scripture to carry Memorise Matthew 28:19-20: "Go therefore and make disciples of all the nations... teaching them to observe all things that I have commanded you." Let "make disciples," not merely "make converts," shape your understanding of the mission.
