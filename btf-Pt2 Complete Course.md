# Lesson 01: Self-Centredness

*Lesson 1 of 16  |  Section A: Mind and Word*


### Opening Question

**Think back to the last argument that really got under your skin. Not the biggest one, the one that stung. What exactly were you defending?**


### Key Scripture

**Proverbs 13:10**
> *By pride comes nothing but strife, But with the well-advised is wisdom.*

*Read it twice. Notice what the verse does not say: it does not say strife comes from difficult people, unfair treatment or bad circumstances.*


### Core Truth

**Strife has only one root, and it is not what happens to you; it is self sitting at the centre of your life. Peace begins where self is dethroned and Christ is Lord.**


### Bible Teaching


Every quarrel feels like it is about something: money, tone of voice, being overlooked, being wronged. This lesson makes an uncomfortable claim from Scripture: beneath every one of those triggers sits a single root, and it lives in us, not in our circumstances.


#### Where strife actually comes from


**Proverbs 13:10**
> *By pride comes nothing but strife, But with the well-advised is wisdom.*


Pride here is not merely arrogance or boasting. It is the habit of standing at the centre of your own world and measuring every situation by how it affects you. From that position, every slight becomes an attack and every disagreement becomes a threat, because the self on the throne must be defended.


> **Caution:** Low self-esteem is not the opposite of pride; it is the same self-centredness seen from the other side. Whether self is inflated or deflated, it is still the thing being stared at. The cure for both is not a better opinion of yourself but a smaller preoccupation with yourself.


**Proverbs 17:14**
> *The beginning of strife is like releasing water; Therefore stop contention before a quarrel starts.*


#### The argument on the road


**Mark 9:33–35**
> *Then He came to Capernaum. And when He was in the house He asked them, "What was it you disputed among yourselves on the road?" But they kept silent, for on the road they had disputed among themselves who would be the greatest. And He sat down, called the twelve, and said to them, "If anyone desires to be first, he shall be last of all and servant of all."*


The disciples went silent because they knew. The argument had felt important on the road; in the presence of Jesus it was suddenly embarrassing. Notice that Jesus does not rebuke the desire to be great. He redefines greatness entirely: it is measured by the direction you face, toward others or toward yourself.


> **Note:** The same argument breaks out again at the Last Supper (Luke 22:24–27), hours before the cross. Jesus ends it with His own self-description: "Yet I am among you as the One who serves." If the Lord of glory took the serving position, no follower of His can claim the centre.


#### The most humble man's response


**Numbers 12:2–3**
> *So they said, "Has the LORD indeed spoken only through Moses? Has He not spoken through us also?" And the LORD heard it. (Now the man Moses was very humble, more than all men who were on the face of the earth.)*


Miriam and Aaron criticise Moses publicly, and the man Scripture calls the most humble on the face of the earth does not defend himself. God defends him, and Moses ends up interceding for his critics. Humility is not weakness or a poor self-image; it is freedom from self-reference. Moses could absorb criticism without strife because his identity was not the thing under attack.


> **Going Deeper:** Meekness in Scripture is strength under God's hand, never spinelessness. Moses confronted Pharaoh and shattered idols; what he refused to do was fight for his own reputation. The self-centred person cannot tell the difference between defending the truth and defending themselves. The humble person can.


#### Dethroning self


**Galatians 2:20**
> *I have been crucified with Christ; it is no longer I who live, but Christ lives in me; and the life which I now live in the flesh I live by faith in the Son of God, who loved me and gave Himself for me.*


The gospel answer to self-centredness is not self-improvement but a death and a replacement. The believer's old self has already been dealt with at the cross; strife loses its fuel when we live from that fact rather than fighting to protect a self God has already crucified and re-created in Christ.


**Matthew 7:12**
> *Therefore, whatever you want men to do to you, do also to them, for this is the Law and the Prophets.*


> **Practical Tip:** In your next disagreement, before you respond, ask one question silently: what am I defending right now? If the honest answer is self, Proverbs 17:14 applies. Stop the water before the dam breaks.


### Key Discovery Questions


1. Proverbs 13:10 locates the source of strife in pride alone. Where do you usually locate it when you are in the middle of one?

2. The disciples went silent when Jesus asked what they had argued about (Mark 9:33–34). Which of your recent arguments would you least want Jesus to ask about, and what does that silence tell you?

3. When Moses was criticised he did not defend himself (Numbers 12:2–3). What do you defend first when criticised, and why that particular thing?

4. If the old self has been crucified with Christ (Galatians 2:20), what exactly comes back to life in the moment you take offence?

5. Think of one person you are currently in tension with. Applying Matthew 7:12, what would you want from you if you were them?


### Group Discussion Questions


1. Jesus did not condemn the desire to be great; He redefined greatness as serving (Mark 9:35). Why do you think He redefined rather than rebuked?

2. Is low self-esteem a form of pride? Talk it through honestly.

3. Where is the line between healthy self-respect and self-centredness?

4. Proverbs 17:14 says to stop contention before the quarrel starts. What does that look like in practice at home, at work and at church?

5. Think of someone you know whose life is marked by unusual peace. What do you notice about how they handle being wronged?


### Application

Self-centredness is not defeated by trying harder to be nice; it is displaced by a bigger centre. Consider how a dethroned self changes each of these areas.


- **Personal life.** In your next disagreement, pause before replying and ask silently: what am I defending right now? If the answer is self, choose to lay it down. Memorise Proverbs 13:10 this week so the diagnosis is always to hand: "By pride comes nothing but strife, But with the well-advised is wisdom."

- **Church community.** Serve someone this week who cannot repay you and whom you find difficult, deliberately and without mentioning it to anyone. Agape service done in secret starves self-centredness of the recognition it feeds on.

- **Difficult seasons.** When criticism comes, follow Moses rather than instinct: refuse to rush to your own defence, and let God defend you. Read Luke 22:24–27 each morning this week; it takes under a minute and resets which way you are facing.


### Revision Cards


- **Q:** What is the sole source of strife? (Proverbs 13:10)  
  **A:** "By pride comes nothing but strife, But with the well-advised is wisdom." Proverbs 13:10

- **Q:** How did Jesus redefine greatness on the road to Capernaum? (Mark 9:35)  
  **A:** "If anyone desires to be first, he shall be last of all and servant of all." Mark 9:35

- **Q:** How did Moses respond to public criticism? (Numbers 12:2–3)  
  **A:** He did not defend himself; God defended him, and Moses interceded for his critics. Scripture calls him more humble than all men on the face of the earth.

- **Q:** What has happened to the believer's old self? (Galatians 2:20)  
  **A:** "I have been crucified with Christ; it is no longer I who live, but Christ lives in me; and the life which I now live in the flesh I live by faith in the Son of God, who loved me and gave Himself for me." Galatians 2:20

- **Q:** What is the everyday antidote to self-centredness? (Matthew 7:12)  
  **A:** "Therefore, whatever you want men to do to you, do also to them, for this is the Law and the Prophets." Matthew 7:12

- **Q:** When should contention be stopped? (Proverbs 17:14)  
  **A:** "The beginning of strife is like releasing water; Therefore stop contention before a quarrel starts." Proverbs 17:14

# Lesson 02: How to Meditate on God's Word

*Lesson 2 of 16  |  Section A: Mind and Word*


### Opening Question

**When you read the Bible, what usually happens next? Be honest: does it stay with you through the day, or is it gone by breakfast?**


### Key Scripture

**Joshua 1:8**
> *This Book of the Law shall not depart from your mouth, but you shall meditate in it day and night, that you may observe to do according to all that is written in it. For then you will make your way prosperous, and then you will have good success.*

*This is the only place in Scripture where God directly promises prosperity and good success, and both are attached to one practice: meditation.*


### Core Truth

**Meditation is not emptying your mind; it is filling it with God's Word and turning that Word over until you meet the God behind it.**


### Bible Teaching


Biblical meditation has nothing to do with emptying the mind. The Hebrew word means to muse over, to ponder, to plan in the mind, to purpose; its Greek counterpart carries the idea of revolving a thing in your thoughts, even to imagine. Meditation is reading slowly enough for the Word to start reading you.


#### Why meditate at all


**Psalm 1:2–3**
> *But his delight is in the law of the LORD, And in His law he meditates day and night. He shall be like a tree Planted by the rivers of water, That brings forth its fruit in its season, Whose leaf also shall not wither; And whatever he does shall prosper.*


Scripture gives meditation two great purposes. The first is the renewing of the mind: pondering correct knowledge until it displaces wrong thinking. The second is greater still: contacting the God who stands behind His Word, through prayer, praise and contemplation. The tree in Psalm 1 does not strain to bear fruit; it is simply planted where the water is.


> **Caution:** It is possible to study words about God and never meet Him in them. Jesus asked, "But why do you call Me 'Lord, Lord,' and not do the things which I say?" (Luke 6:46). Meditation that never becomes obedience and relationship is only word study.


#### Four ways in


There is no single required method; Scripture invites several. A topical study follows one subject through the Bible, defining the word, tracing its roots and pondering every verse in context. An expositional study walks through one book verse by verse until you know its argument like your own street. A word study takes a single term, believe, Lord, justify, and digs until its weight is felt. A paragraph study treats a passage as one unit of thought and asks where the writer turns and why.


> **Practical Tip:** Notice punctuation as you read, especially questions. Every question in Scripture was asked for a reason; asking why this question, here, to these people is one of the fastest ways into a passage.


#### Hiding the Word


**Psalm 119:11**
> *Your word I have hidden in my heart, That I might not sin against You.*


What is merely read can be forgotten; what is meditated on is hidden, stored where temptation and trouble cannot easily reach it. This is why Joshua 1:8 joins the mouth, the mind and the hands: speak it, ponder it, do it. The promised prosperity is not magic; it is the natural fruit of a life continually steered by God's thoughts.


**2 Timothy 2:15**
> *Be diligent to present yourself approved to God, a worker who does not need to be ashamed, rightly dividing the word of truth.*


#### The God behind the Word


**Psalm 19:14**
> *Let the words of my mouth and the meditation of my heart Be acceptable in Your sight, O LORD, my strength and my Redeemer.*


David does not ask for an acceptable technique but for an acceptable heart. The end of meditation is never information; it is communion. When pondering a verse turns into speaking with its Author, meditation has done its work.


> **Going Deeper:** Meditation seeks God behind the Word, not merely the meaning of the words. The rabbis compared Scripture to a fire and a hammer (Jeremiah 23:29); analysis alone handles the hammer but never feels the heat. Ponder until the text becomes prayer.


### Key Discovery Questions


1. Joshua 1:8 joins mouth, mind and action: speak it, meditate on it, do it. Which of the three is weakest in your current practice, and what would strengthen it?

2. Psalm 1 pictures the meditating believer as a tree planted by water. What season are you in right now, and what does being "planted" look like for you this month?

3. Psalm 119:11 speaks of hiding the Word in the heart. What is the difference, in your experience, between reading a verse and hiding it?

4. Of the four methods described (topical, expositional, word study, paragraph study), which have you actually tried, and which one will you attempt this week?

5. Luke 6:46 asks why we call Jesus Lord and do not do what He says. Where has your Bible reading produced knowledge but not yet obedience?


### Group Discussion Questions


1. What has been your honest experience of Bible reading: duty, delight, confusion, habit? What made the difference when it came alive?

2. Biblical meditation fills the mind while other kinds of meditation empty it. Why does that distinction matter?

3. Joshua 1:8 attaches prosperity and success to meditation. What do you think that promise does and does not mean?

4. Share one verse that has stayed with you for years. Why do you think that one took root?

5. What practically stops us from meditating: time, distraction, not knowing how? What has helped anyone in the group?


### Application

Meditation is a practice, not a talent. It grows by minutes, not by leaps. Consider how it lands in each of these areas.


- **Personal life.** Choose one verse this week, Joshua 1:8 itself is a fine start, and carry it through the day: read it at breakfast, speak it aloud at midday, ponder it before sleep. One verse held all day beats a chapter forgotten by nine.

- **Church community.** In your group or with a friend, try a paragraph study together on a short passage. Two people asking "why does the writer turn here?" will see more than either alone, and you will be practising 1 Corinthians 14:26 edification as you do it.

- **Difficult seasons.** When the mind is too tired or troubled to study, do not force analysis. Take one short line, "You will keep him in perfect peace, Whose mind is stayed on You", and simply repeat it slowly to God. Meditation in a storm is anchoring, not achieving.


### Revision Cards


- **Q:** What does God promise to the one who meditates day and night? (Joshua 1:8)  
  **A:** "For then you will make your way prosperous, and then you will have good success." Joshua 1:8

- **Q:** What is the meditating believer compared to in Psalm 1? (Psalm 1:2–3)  
  **A:** "He shall be like a tree Planted by the rivers of water, That brings forth its fruit in its season, Whose leaf also shall not wither; And whatever he does shall prosper." Psalm 1:3

- **Q:** Why is the Word hidden in the heart? (Psalm 119:11)  
  **A:** "Your word I have hidden in my heart, That I might not sin against You." Psalm 119:11

- **Q:** What does biblical meditation actually mean?  
  **A:** To muse over, ponder, plan in the mind, purpose or intend; revolving God's Word in the mind until it shapes thought and action. Filling the mind, never emptying it.

- **Q:** What are the four methods of meditation taught in this lesson?  
  **A:** Topical study (one subject across Scripture), expositional study (one book verse by verse), word study (one term in depth) and paragraph study (one unit of thought).

- **Q:** What is the ultimate aim of meditation? (Psalm 19:14)  
  **A:** "Let the words of my mouth and the meditation of my heart Be acceptable in Your sight, O LORD, my strength and my Redeemer." Psalm 19:14

# Lesson 03: Renewing the Mind

*Lesson 3 of 16  |  Section A: Mind and Word*


### Opening Question

**What thought have you replayed most this week? If your closest friend could hear your inner monologue, what would surprise them?**


### Key Scripture

**Romans 12:2**
> *And do not be conformed to this world, but be transformed by the renewing of your mind, that you may prove what is that good and acceptable and perfect will of God.*

*Transformation is promised here, but notice where it happens: not first in circumstances or behaviour, but in the mind.*


### Core Truth

**You cannot always choose the thoughts that arrive, but you always choose the thoughts that stay. Transformation happens where thoughts are dwelt on.**


### Bible Teaching


The battle of the Christian life is fought closer to home than most believers realise: not first in circumstances, habits or relationships, but in the six inches between the ears. Scripture insists that we have real agency over what we dwell on, and that what we dwell on is what we become.


#### You can choose your thoughts


**Philippians 4:8**
> *Finally, brethren, whatever things are true, whatever things are noble, whatever things are just, whatever things are pure, whatever things are lovely, whatever things are of good report, if there is any virtue and if there is anything praiseworthy—meditate on these things.*


This command would be cruel if it were impossible. Intrusive thoughts arise unbidden; Paul knew that struggle personally (Romans 7:22–23). But arrival is not residence. The instruction to meditate on these things assumes the believer holds the keys to what settles in.


**Proverbs 23:7**
> *For as he thinks in his heart, so is he. "Eat and drink!" he says to you, But his heart is not with you.*


#### A living sacrifice and a renewed mind


**Romans 12:1–2**
> *I beseech you therefore, brethren, by the mercies of God, that you present your bodies a living sacrifice, holy, acceptable to God, which is your reasonable service. And do not be conformed to this world, but be transformed by the renewing of your mind, that you may prove what is that good and acceptable and perfect will of God.*


Two commitments stand together: present the body, renew the mind. Like a spacecraft on course for the moon, the renewed mind is not set once and forgotten; it makes constant small corrections, always steering back toward the goal. Conformity to the world happens by drift; transformation happens by deliberate, repeated adjustment.


> **Note:** Renewal is not suppression. Trying hard not to think a thought fixes attention on it; tell someone not to picture a pink elephant and watch what happens. The biblical method is replacement: turn the mind actively to what God says, and the destructive thought loses its grip because attention, its oxygen, is gone.


#### Life and peace, or death


**Romans 8:6**
> *For to be carnally minded is death, but to be spiritually minded is life and peace.*


This is not poetry; it is diagnosis. A mind set on the flesh produces death in slow instalments: anxiety, resentment, despair. A mind set on the Spirit produces life and peace as surely as a planted field produces its crop. The real spiritual warfare is fought over where your mind habitually rests.


> **Caution:** Many believers try to fight the enemy with words while surrendering to him in thought. Resisting the devil (James 4:7) begins with refusing his suggestions dwelling space, not with shouting at him while rehearsing his lies.


#### Kept in perfect peace


**Isaiah 26:3**
> *You will keep him in perfect peace, Whose mind is stayed on You, Because he trusts in You.*


The promise is staggering: perfect peace, kept by God Himself. The condition is a stayed mind, one that returns to God as its resting place through the day. When a wrong thought arises, recognise it, turn to what God has said, and draw near to Him rather than merely analysing the thought. He has promised to draw near in return (James 4:8).


> **Practical Tip:** Keep a short list of what God declares about you in Christ: loved, forgiven, righteous, kept. When a destructive thought loops, read the list aloud. You are not suppressing the thought; you are evicting it by occupancy.


### Key Discovery Questions


1. Philippians 4:8 gives eight tests for what deserves your attention. Run yesterday's dominant thoughts through them honestly: how many pass?

2. Romans 12:1–2 joins presenting the body with renewing the mind. Why do you think Paul requires both, and which comes harder to you?

3. Romans 8:6 says carnal mindedness is death and spiritual mindedness is life and peace. Where have you tasted each side of that verse personally?

4. Isaiah 26:3 promises perfect peace to the mind stayed on God. What most often unstays your mind, and what brings it back?

5. The replacement principle says thoughts are evicted by occupancy, not suppression. What specific truth of God will you keep ready for your most recurring negative thought?


### Group Discussion Questions


1. Is it genuinely possible to choose your thoughts? Share experiences on both sides.

2. The world conforms us by drift, without asking permission. Where do you see that drift working on believers today?

3. Why does suppressing a thought strengthen it while replacing it weakens it?

4. What is the difference between renewing the mind and positive thinking?

5. Acts 17:11 commends the Bereans for testing everything against Scripture. How would our thinking change if we did that daily?


### Application

The mind is renewed the way a course is corrected: by many small, deliberate adjustments. Consider these areas.


- **Personal life.** Choose your most persistent negative thought pattern. Write beside it one thing God declares in Scripture that answers it, and every time the thought arrives this week, answer it aloud with the verse. Track it: eviction by occupancy is measurable.

- **Church community.** Guard the collective mind as well as your own. Steer conversations away from rehearsing grievances and toward Philippians 4:8 territory. A group renews minds together or conforms together; there is no neutral.

- **Difficult seasons.** In crisis, do not attempt deep study; keep the mind stayed with short returns. Set three fixed moments a day to speak Isaiah 26:3 back to God. Peace is His keeping, not your achieving; your part is the returning.


### Revision Cards


- **Q:** Where does transformation happen? (Romans 12:2)  
  **A:** "And do not be conformed to this world, but be transformed by the renewing of your mind, that you may prove what is that good and acceptable and perfect will of God." Romans 12:2

- **Q:** What eight things deserve your meditation? (Philippians 4:8)  
  **A:** "Whatever things are true, whatever things are noble, whatever things are just, whatever things are pure, whatever things are lovely, whatever things are of good report, if there is any virtue and if there is anything praiseworthy—meditate on these things." Philippians 4:8

- **Q:** What do the two mindsets produce? (Romans 8:6)  
  **A:** "For to be carnally minded is death, but to be spiritually minded is life and peace." Romans 8:6

- **Q:** What is promised to the mind stayed on God? (Isaiah 26:3)  
  **A:** "You will keep him in perfect peace, Whose mind is stayed on You, Because he trusts in You." Isaiah 26:3

- **Q:** As a man thinks in his heart… (Proverbs 23:7)  
  **A:** "For as he thinks in his heart, so is he." Proverbs 23:7

- **Q:** What is the replacement principle?  
  **A:** Thoughts are not defeated by suppression but by replacement: turning the mind actively to God's truth removes the attention a destructive thought needs to survive.

# Lesson 04: The Importance of Christ's Church

*Lesson 4 of 16  |  Section B: Church and Authority*


### Opening Question

**When you hear the word "church", what picture forms first: a building, a Sunday service, or people? Where do you think that picture came from?**


### Key Scripture

**Hebrews 10:24–25**
> *And let us consider one another in order to stir up love and good works, not forsaking the assembling of ourselves together, as is the manner of some, but exhorting one another, and so much the more as you see the Day approaching.*

*Notice the purpose attached to assembling: not attendance for its own sake, but considering one another, stirring up love, exhorting.*


### Core Truth

**The church is not a building you attend but a people you belong to; wherever believers gather in Jesus' name to build one another up, the church is meeting.**


### Bible Teaching


A pastor once asked why twenty flourishing home Bible studies, where people were being saved and discipled weekly, did not count as church because the people were not in a Sunday service. The question exposes a confusion as old as it is common: what exactly is the church?


#### What the word actually means


The New Testament word for church, ecclesia, means an assembly of people gathered to worship, pray, praise and look to God. It never once refers to a building. In Scripture the word stretches from a handful of believers in a house (Romans 16:5) to all the believers of a city (Acts 11:22) to whole regions, but the constant is always people, never premises.


**Matthew 18:20**
> *For where two or three are gathered together in My name, I am there in the midst of them.*


> **Note:** The early church met overwhelmingly in homes: the church in the house of Priscilla and Aquila (Romans 16:3–5; 1 Corinthians 16:19), in the house of Nymphas (Colossians 4:15), in the house of Philemon (Philemon 2), and daily "in the temple, and in every house" (Acts 5:42; 20:20). Buildings came centuries later; the church did not wait for them.


#### What the gathering is for


**Acts 2:42**
> *And they continued steadfastly in the apostles' doctrine and fellowship, in the breaking of bread, and in prayers.*


The assembly exists for edification: building one another up. When the Corinthian believers met, everyone brought something.


**1 Corinthians 14:26**
> *How is it then, brethren? Whenever you come together, each of you has a psalm, has a teaching, has a tongue, has a revelation, has an interpretation. Let all things be done for edification.*


This was participation, not performance: gifts exercised, words weighed carefully (1 Corinthians 14:29), every member functioning. God has set gifts in the church, apostles, prophets, teachers, miracles, healings, helps, administrations, tongues (1 Corinthians 12:28), and gifts unexercised are gifts wasted.


> **Caution:** Passive attendance can coexist with total isolation. It is possible to sit in rows for years, watch the front, and never once be considered, stirred up or exhorted by anyone. Hebrews 10:24–25 is not fulfilled by being in the room; it is fulfilled by one-another ministry.


#### The living cycle


**Acts 2:47**
> *Praising God and having favor with all the people. And the Lord added to the church daily those who were being saved.*


The early pattern was a breathing rhythm: believers scattered to share their faith, the Lord added the saved not to buildings but to His people, the people gathered to exercise gifts and encourage one another, then scattered again with the Word. Evangelism fed the gathering; the gathering fuelled evangelism.


#### Why you cannot survive alone


You were not saved into isolation; you were placed into a body. The believer who withdraws from gathering cuts themselves off from the gifts God placed in others for their sake, and withholds the gift God placed in them for others. Even two or three meeting regularly in Jesus' name is church, and every believer needs it. The church also proved its faith by practical care: feeding widows daily (Acts 6:1), visiting orphans and widows in affliction (James 1:27), and supporting those who preach the gospel (1 Corinthians 9:14).


> **Practical Tip:** Stop asking "did I go to church this week?" and start asking "did I build anyone up this week, and did I let anyone build me?" The second question is the biblical one, whatever the size or shape of the gathering.


### Key Discovery Questions


1. Hebrews 10:24–25 commands us not to forsake assembling, and attaches a purpose: exhorting one another. When did you last leave a gathering having genuinely built someone up? What happened?

2. Acts 2:42 lists four things the early church continued in steadfastly. Which of the four is strongest in your experience of church, and which is thinnest?

3. 1 Corinthians 14:26 describes a gathering where everyone contributes. What do you have, a psalm, a teaching, an encouragement, that currently goes unshared?

4. Matthew 18:20 promises Jesus' presence to two or three gathered in His name. Does your practice reflect that belief, or do you reserve "real church" for larger settings?

5. James 1:27 defines pure religion by care for orphans and widows. Who in your circle fits that description, and what would visiting them look like this month?


### Group Discussion Questions


1. What did you grow up believing "church" meant, and how has this lesson confirmed or challenged it?

2. Why do you think the meaning of ecclesia shifted from people to buildings over the centuries? Does it matter?

3. What are the strengths and dangers of large-gathering church and small home-gathering church? Do we need both?

4. Passive attendance versus participating body: which have you mostly experienced, and what made the difference?

5. The early church shared money and food daily with those in need (Acts 2:44–45; 6:1). What would that spirit look like in our context, realistically?


### Application

Belonging to the body is practised, not felt. Consider what functioning membership looks like in each area.


- **Personal life.** Identify the gift God has placed in you, encouragement, teaching, helps, hospitality, and exercise it deliberately at the next gathering. Go intending to give, not only to receive; 1 Corinthians 14:26 assumes you arrive carrying something.

- **Church community.** Move one relationship from row-level to one-another level this month: share a meal, pray together in a home, study a passage as two or three. The smallest gathering in Jesus' name carries His full presence (Matthew 18:20).

- **Difficult seasons.** When life is hardest is when withdrawal whispers loudest and when the body matters most. Tell two believers the truth about your season and let them carry you; refusing help denies others their ministry and you their gifts.


### Revision Cards


- **Q:** What must we not forsake, and why? (Hebrews 10:25)  
  **A:** "Not forsaking the assembling of ourselves together, as is the manner of some, but exhorting one another, and so much the more as you see the Day approaching." Hebrews 10:25

- **Q:** What did the early church continue steadfastly in? (Acts 2:42)  
  **A:** "And they continued steadfastly in the apostles' doctrine and fellowship, in the breaking of bread, and in prayers." Acts 2:42

- **Q:** What happens when even two or three gather in Jesus' name? (Matthew 18:20)  
  **A:** "For where two or three are gathered together in My name, I am there in the midst of them." Matthew 18:20

- **Q:** What does the word ecclesia mean?  
  **A:** An assembly of people gathered for worship, prayer, praise and looking to God. In the New Testament it always means people, from a house group to a city's believers, and never a building.

- **Q:** What was every believer expected to bring to the gathering? (1 Corinthians 14:26)  
  **A:** "Whenever you come together, each of you has a psalm, has a teaching, has a tongue, has a revelation, has an interpretation. Let all things be done for edification." 1 Corinthians 14:26

- **Q:** Who added people to the church, and to what? (Acts 2:47)  
  **A:** "And the Lord added to the church daily those who were being saved." Acts 2:47

# Lesson 05: Deliverance

*Lesson 5 of 16  |  Section B: Church and Authority*


### Opening Question

**Does the devil feel more real to you than the authority you have over him, or less? What has shaped that instinct?**


### Key Scripture

**1 John 3:8**
> *He who sins is of the devil, for the devil has sinned from the beginning. For this purpose the Son of God was manifested, that He might destroy the works of the devil.*

*The verse states the purpose of the incarnation in one clause. Whatever the devil builds, the Son came to demolish.*


### Core Truth

**Jesus came to destroy the works of the devil, and He has armed His people with His Word and His name; the believer fights from victory, not for it.**


### Bible Teaching


Roughly a quarter of Jesus' recorded ministry involved setting people free from demonic oppression. Many believers quietly assume such things belong to other times or other places; Scripture, and the experience of anyone long in ministry, says otherwise. The good news is that this subject, rightly seen, produces confidence rather than fear.


#### What Jesus came to do


**Acts 10:38**
> *How God anointed Jesus of Nazareth with the Holy Spirit and with power, who went about doing good and healing all who were oppressed by the devil, for God was with Him.*


Oppression is the devil's work; destroying it is Jesus' stated purpose (1 John 3:8). Every deliverance in the Gospels is a demonstration of a kingdom changing hands.


**Colossians 1:13**
> *He has delivered us from the power of darkness and conveyed us into the kingdom of the Son of His love.*


> **Note:** The believer's deliverance is first of all positional: you have already been transferred out of darkness' jurisdiction into Christ's kingdom. The enemy's only remaining weapons against a transferred citizen are deception and intimidation, which is precisely why he fears the Word that exposes him.


#### The sword of the Spirit


**Ephesians 6:17**
> *And take the helmet of salvation, and the sword of the Spirit, which is the word of God.*


Of the whole armour of God, one piece is offensive: the Word. When Jesus faced Satan in the wilderness He did not argue, reason or panic; three times He answered "It is written" (Matthew 4:10), and the tempter left. What worked for the Son of God in person is the weapon He hands His people. Spoken Scripture does what shouting never will.


> **Caution:** This battle is real but it is not against people: "we do not wrestle against flesh and blood" (Ephesians 6:12). The moment spiritual warfare turns into suspicion or fear of human beings, it has left the biblical rails.


#### Why the enemy resists the Word


Ask anyone who has tried to establish a habit of Scripture and prayer: the interference is strangely specific. The enemy does not mind religious activity; he minds believers knowing God through His Word, because informed believers are dangerous to him. Resistance around Bible study is not a sign of failure but of significance.


**James 4:7**
> *Therefore submit to God. Resist the devil and he will flee from you.*


The order matters: submit first, resist second. Authority over the enemy flows from being under authority; a life yielded to God can resist with confidence, and the promise is not that the devil might flee but that he will.


#### Authority in Jesus' name


**Luke 10:19**
> *Behold, I give you the authority to trample on serpents and scorpions, and over all the power of the enemy, and nothing shall by any means hurt you.*


Jesus gave His disciples authority to cast out demons in His name (Matthew 10:8; Mark 16:17), and the seventy returned astonished that it worked. The name of Jesus is not a magic word; it is delegated authority, effective in the mouth of those who belong to Him and live under Him. The believer need never be bullied: greater is He who is in you.


> **Practical Tip:** When oppression, fear or dark suggestion presses in, do what Jesus did: answer aloud with written Scripture. Keep Philippians 2:9–11 and James 4:7 ready. Speak them, submit afresh to God, and stand; fleeing is the enemy's job, not yours.

5. Luke 10:19 grants authority over all the power of the enemy. Where do you behave as though that verse were not true, and what would change if you believed it?

3. Why is the Word called the sword of the Spirit rather than our arguments, volume or willpower?

4. Have you experienced strange resistance when trying to establish prayer or Bible reading? What does that suggest?

5. What is the difference between healthy spiritual vigilance and fear? How do we help each other stay on the right side?


### Application

Deliverance truths are lived quietly long before they are needed loudly. Consider these areas.


- **Personal life.** Memorise James 4:7 and keep it loaded: "Therefore submit to God. Resist the devil and he will flee from you." Practise the order daily, submission first, resistance second, and answer dark suggestions aloud with written Scripture rather than debating them in your head.

- **Church community.** Pray regularly for those you are discipling, because resistance clusters around new growth. When a new believer suddenly faces chaos, recognise the pattern, name it soberly without drama, and stand with them in the Word.

- **Difficult seasons.** Under sustained oppression, do not isolate and do not improvise: anchor to your position (Colossians 1:13), speak the Word aloud, and ask mature believers to pray with you. Nothing about the fight is meant to be fought alone.

# Lesson 06: Authority of the Believer

*Lesson 6 of 16  |  Section B: Church and Authority*


### Opening Question

**When you pray about a problem, do you speak as someone asking for help from outside the situation, or as someone given authority inside it? Which feels truer of you?**


### Key Scripture

**Matthew 28:18**
> *And Jesus came and spoke to them, saying, "All authority has been given to Me in heaven and on earth."*

*The very next word is "Go" (verse 19). All authority claimed, then immediately delegated into the mission of His people.*


### Core Truth

**Satan's only real weapon is deception; Christ holds all authority and shares it with His people, so the believer who knows the truth cannot be ruled by a defeated enemy.**


### Bible Teaching


Most fear of the enemy rests on a misunderstanding of his capabilities. Scripture presents Satan not as God's opposite but as a defeated usurper whose one remaining strategy is deception. Trace the story of authority through the Bible and the fear drains out of it.


#### Dominion given


**Genesis 1:26**
> *Then God said, "Let Us make man in Our image, according to Our likeness; let them have dominion over the fish of the sea, over the birds of the air, and over the cattle, over all the earth and over every creeping thing that creeps on the earth."*


**Psalm 115:16**
> *The heaven, even the heavens, are the LORD's; But the earth He has given to the children of men.*


God retained ownership of everything but delegated the running of the earth to mankind. That delegation was real: God gave humanity genuine authority to rule the domain He had made.


#### Authority surrendered


Notice how Satan came in Genesis 3: not with force but with a question, through a serpent described as cunning (Genesis 3:1). He could not take Adam and Eve's authority; he could only talk them into yielding it. When they obeyed his word above God's, mankind handed its delegated rule to the deceiver, and he became "the god of this age" (2 Corinthians 4:4), able to offer the kingdoms of the world in the wilderness temptation (Matthew 4:8–9).


> **Note:** Satan's method has never changed because it is all he has. He fires lies; we supply the ammunition by believing them. He gained authority by deception in Eden and holds ground in a believer's life only the same way, which is why Jesus said the truth itself makes you free (John 8:32).


#### Authority recovered


**Matthew 28:18**
> *And Jesus came and spoke to them, saying, "All authority has been given to Me in heaven and on earth."*


Jesus, God become man, succeeded where Adam fell. He refused every deception, went to the cross, and rose holding all authority in heaven and on earth. And He did not keep it to Himself: "Go therefore and make disciples" (verse 19). The risen Christ exercises His earthly authority through His body. Believers now share authority with Him, and what is shared with Christ can never again be wholly surrendered as Adam surrendered it.


> **Going Deeper:** Ephesians 1:19 speaks of "the exceeding greatness of His power toward us who believe", the same power that raised Christ and seated Him far above all principality and power. The believer's authority is not self-generated; it is seated-with-Christ authority, exercised in His name.


#### Fighting the real battle


**2 Corinthians 10:4–5**
> *For the weapons of our warfare are not carnal but mighty in God for pulling down strongholds, casting down arguments and every high thing that exalts itself against the knowledge of God, bringing every thought into captivity to the obedience of Christ.*


If the enemy's weapon is deception, the battlefield is the mind and the warfare is truth. Strongholds are built of arguments and imaginations, not bricks. Exercising authority looks less like shouting at darkness and more like refusing lies dwelling space, taking thoughts captive and standing on what God has said until the intimidation collapses.


> **Practical Tip:** Next time a fear or accusation loops in your mind, treat it as an enemy suggestion rather than a fact. Ask: what has God actually said about this? Then take the thought captive to that word, out loud if necessary. That is the authority of the believer in daily use.


### Key Discovery Questions


1. Psalm 115:16 says God gave the earth to the children of men. What does it change to see yourself as delegated ruler rather than helpless spectator?

2. Satan gained authority in Eden purely by deception (Genesis 3:1). Which of his suggestions have you been treating as facts?

3. Matthew 28:18–19 moves straight from "All authority has been given to Me" to "Go therefore". What does that sequence tell you about how Christ intends His authority to operate?

4. 2 Corinthians 10:5 speaks of taking every thought captive. Describe what that would practically look like for your most persistent anxious thought.

5. John 8:32 says the truth makes you free. Name one area where knowing the truth has already freed you, and one where freedom is still pending.


### Group Discussion Questions


1. Why do you think Satan chose deception rather than force in Eden? What does that reveal about his actual power?

2. How do inflated views of the devil's power dishonour Christ? How does dismissing him entirely do the same?

3. What is the difference between authority and power? Why does a police officer's raised hand stop a lorry?

4. Where do believers today most commonly "supply the ammunition" for the enemy's lies?

5. How would our praying change if we truly believed we are seated with Christ far above all principality and power?


### Application

Authority unused is authority in name only. Consider how shared authority with Christ operates in each area.


- **Personal life.** Audit your self-talk for a week. Each time you catch a thought that contradicts what God has said about you, name it as a suggestion, refuse it, and replace it with the truth aloud. This is 2 Corinthians 10:5 in working clothes.

- **Church community.** Speak truth to one another deliberately. When a brother or sister rehearses the enemy's script about themselves, gently put God's script back in their hands. The body enforces Christ's authority corporately, one renewed conversation at a time.

- **Difficult seasons.** When circumstances scream that darkness is winning, return to the throne room: Christ holds all authority now, not after things improve. Pray from victory, not toward it, and let Matthew 28:18 set the tone of your requests.

# Lesson 07: Healing is in the Atonement

*Lesson 7 of 16  |  Section C: Healing and Relationships*


### Opening Question

**When you pray for someone's healing, what do you honestly expect to happen? Where did that level of expectation come from?**


### Key Scripture

**Isaiah 53:4–5**
> *Surely He has borne our griefs And carried our sorrows; Yet we esteemed Him stricken, Smitten by God, and afflicted. But He was wounded for our transgressions, He was bruised for our iniquities; The chastisement for our peace was upon Him, And by His stripes we are healed.*

*Seven hundred years before the cross, the prophet lists what the Servant would carry. Read the list slowly: griefs, sorrows, transgressions, iniquities, peace, healing.*


### Core Truth

**At the cross Jesus carried sins and sicknesses together; healing is not a lucky extra but a purchased provision of the atonement.**


### Bible Teaching


Did the cross deal only with sin, or with sickness too? The question is not academic; it sets the level of every prayer for healing you will ever pray. Scripture's answer is given by an inspired interpretation of Isaiah's prophecy, made while Jesus was healing real bodies.


#### Matthew's inspired interpretation


**Matthew 8:16–17**
> *When evening had come, they brought to Him many who were demon-possessed. And He cast out the spirits with a word, and healed all who were sick, that it might be fulfilled which was spoken by Isaiah the prophet, saying: "He Himself took our infirmities And bore our sicknesses."*


Matthew, writing under the Spirit, applies Isaiah 53 to physical healing: bodies cured of real diseases, in fulfilment of what the Servant would bear. Whatever else Isaiah 53 means, Scripture itself says it includes the physical. The context resists being spiritualised away, because the healings it describes were not spiritual metaphors; blind eyes opened and fevers left.


> **Note:** Some traditions read "by His stripes we are healed" as emotional or spiritual healing only. Matthew 8:17 makes that reading impossible to sustain: the fulfilment named is the healing of sick bodies. Peter repeats the truth looking back at the cross: "by whose stripes you were healed" (1 Peter 2:24), past tense, accomplished.


#### One word, whole salvation


The Greek word sozo, translated "saved", is used in the New Testament for forgiveness of sins, rescue from danger, deliverance from demons and healing of the body. Salvation in Scripture is a whole-person word. The same James who tells the sick to call the elders promises that "the prayer of faith will save [sozo] the sick, and the Lord will raise him up" (James 5:14–15).


#### Preaching and healing together


**Matthew 10:7–8**
> *And as you go, preach, saying, "The kingdom of heaven is at hand." Heal the sick, cleanse the lepers, raise the dead, cast out demons. Freely you have received, freely give.*


Jesus never sent His messengers with words alone. Preaching and healing travel together in His commissions, including the great one: "they will lay hands on the sick, and they will recover" (Mark 16:18). The kingdom announced is a kingdom demonstrated.


> **Caution:** God is not the author of sickness. Hardship may drive people toward God, but that never makes the hardship His gift; Jesus healed all who came, and everything He did was the visible will of the Father. Do not credit to God what Jesus spent His ministry destroying.


#### Provision and pastoral honesty


Healing is purchased and available; it is not a test of salvation or of love for God. Believers can love God deeply, die unhealed and be welcomed home; nothing in this lesson licenses condemnation of the sick. What the atonement settles is the direction of God's will: He is for your healing, and prayer can be prayed from provision rather than from uncertainty.


> **Practical Tip:** Pray for the sick the way you pray for the lost: on the basis of what the cross has already provided, not as a lottery. "Lord, You bore this" is a very different starting point from "Lord, if You are willing." The leper who prayed the second way was answered: "I am willing" (Mark 1:41).


### Key Discovery Questions


1. Isaiah 53:4–5 lists what the Servant carried. Which item on that list have you never consciously thanked Him for carrying?

2. Matthew 8:16–17 applies Isaiah's prophecy to physical healing. Does your theology of the cross include your body? How can you tell?

3. 1 Peter 2:24 puts healing in the past tense: "were healed". What is the difference between praying for something to be given and receiving something provided?

4. Jesus joined preaching and healing in Matthew 10:7–8. Why do you think the two belong together, and what happens when the church separates them?

5. Where has fear of disappointment quietly lowered your expectation in prayer? What would praying from provision sound like this week?


### Group Discussion Questions


1. What were you taught, explicitly or by silence, about whether God still heals? How has it shaped your praying?

2. Why is Matthew 8:17 so decisive in the debate about what Isaiah 53 covers?

3. How do we hold together bold faith for healing and honest love for those not yet healed? What does getting that balance wrong look like on each side?

4. If God is not the author of sickness, how should we speak about illness in our prayers and conversations?

5. What would change in our church if we genuinely believed healing is in the atonement?


### Application

Provision changes posture. Consider how a purchased healing reshapes each of these areas.


- **Personal life.** Memorise 1 Peter 2:24 and pray it over your own health needs this week: "by whose stripes you were healed." Let the past tense of the verse set the tense of your prayers.

- **Church community.** When someone asks for prayer for sickness, actually pray, there and then, on the basis of the atonement. James 5:14–15 assumes the church is a place where the sick are prayed for expectantly, not merely sympathised with.

- **Difficult seasons.** If healing tarries, refuse both despair and self-accusation. Keep receiving prayer, keep the Word before your eyes, and rest your identity on what is certain: the same cross that carried your sins carried your sicknesses, and God's will toward you is good.

  **A:** "But He was wounded for our transgressions, He was bruised for our iniquities; The chastisement for our peace was upon Him, And by His stripes we are healed." Isaiah 53:5

- **Q:** How did Matthew interpret Isaiah's prophecy? (Matthew 8:17)  
  **A:** "That it might be fulfilled which was spoken by Isaiah the prophet, saying: 'He Himself took our infirmities And bore our sicknesses.'" Matthew 8:17

- **Q:** What tense does Peter use for healing? (1 Peter 2:24)  
  **A:** "Who Himself bore our sins in His own body on the tree, that we, having died to sins, might live for righteousness—by whose stripes you were healed." 1 Peter 2:24

- **Q:** What does the Greek word sozo cover?  
  **A:** The New Testament word for "saved" covers forgiveness of sins, rescue, deliverance and physical healing. Salvation is a whole-person word: spirit, soul and body.

  **A:** "Heal the sick, cleanse the lepers, raise the dead, cast out demons. Freely you have received, freely give." Matthew 10:8

  **A:** "And the prayer of faith will save the sick, and the Lord will raise him up. And if he has committed sins, he will be forgiven." James 5:15

# Lesson 08: Hindrances to Healing

*Lesson 8 of 16  |  Section C: Healing and Relationships*


### Opening Question

**Have you ever prayed for healing that did not come? What conclusion did you quietly draw about God, about yourself, or about prayer?**


### Key Scripture

**Hosea 4:6**
> *My people are destroyed for lack of knowledge. Because you have rejected knowledge, I also will reject you from being priest for Me; Because you have forgotten the law of your God, I also will forget your children.*

*The destruction described here is not caused by God's unwillingness but by His people's ignorance. That distinction carries the whole lesson.*


### Core Truth

**God's willingness to heal is settled at the cross; where healing is hindered, the blockage lies in ignorance, sin's inroads or unbelief, never in God's reluctance.**


### Bible Teaching


If healing is in the atonement, why is not everyone healed? The honest question deserves an honest biblical answer, and the answer Scripture gives is liberating: the variable is never God's willingness. Several identifiable things can hinder, and every one of them can be addressed.


#### Ignorance


A person can perish under a law of gravity they never heard of; ignorance exempts no one from consequences. Likewise, believers who do not know that healing is provided cannot receive what they do not know exists. Whole generations have died early not because God withheld but because knowledge was lacking, and Hosea names that tragedy precisely: destroyed for lack of knowledge.


> **Note:** This truth removes blame from God without dumping it on the sufferer. A person who prayed in the dark and lost someone did not fail; they simply had not been taught. The response to new knowledge is never guilt about the past but faith for the present.


#### Sin's inroads


**John 9:2–3**
> *And His disciples asked Him, saying, "Rabbi, who sinned, this man or his parents, that he was born blind?" Jesus answered, "Neither this man nor his parents sinned, but that the works of God should be revealed in him."*


Scripture holds two truths in balance. Not all sickness is caused by personal sin; Jesus flatly refused that equation. Yet sin can open a door: after healing one man, Jesus warned, "See, you have been made well. Sin no more, lest a worse thing come upon you" (John 5:14). Yielding to sin yields ground (Romans 6:16), and the thief uses every inch he is given (John 10:10).


> **Caution:** Beware morbid introspection. Some believers turn every symptom into a hunt for hidden guilt and end up believing they are too unworthy to be healed, which is itself a lie. Where there is real sin, repent and move on; the blood cleanses. Endless self-examination is not humility, it is unbelief wearing a religious coat.


#### Unbelief, ours and others'


**Mark 6:5–6**
> *Now He could do no mighty work there, except that He laid His hands on a few sick people and healed them. And He marveled because of their unbelief. Then He went about the villages in a circuit, teaching.*


In Nazareth the limiting factor was not the Healer but the atmosphere. Matthew records that He did not do many mighty works there because of their unbelief (Matthew 13:58). Corporate scepticism can smother what God is willing to do, and He will not force healing on those who refuse to receive it; He honours the freedom He gave.


#### What to do


**James 5:15**
> *And the prayer of faith will save the sick, and the Lord will raise him up. And if he has committed sins, he will be forgiven.*


The path through hindrances is practical: get knowledge, feeding on what Scripture actually says about healing until expectation rises; close sin's doors by simple repentance, and receive forgiveness with the healing; and guard the atmosphere, choosing who prays with you as carefully as Jesus did when He put the wailers out before raising the girl (Mark 5:40). None of these steps earns healing; they remove the debris from a provision already made.


> **Practical Tip:** Build your faith before the crisis, not during it. Read the healing narratives of the Gospels regularly, noting each time Jesus' willingness. Knowledge stored in health is faith available in sickness.


### Key Discovery Questions


1. Hosea 4:6 says God's people are destroyed for lack of knowledge. What did you not know about healing five years ago that you know now, and what difference has it made?

2. Jesus refused the equation "sickness equals personal sin" (John 9:3) yet warned a healed man to sin no more (John 5:14). How do you hold those two texts together?

3. Where does morbid introspection show up in your praying? What would simple repentance followed by confidence look like instead?

4. In Nazareth unbelief limited what Jesus did (Mark 6:5–6). What is the faith atmosphere of your home and church, and what feeds it?

5. James 5:15 joins healing and forgiveness in one prayer. Why do you think God packages them together?


### Group Discussion Questions


1. Which explanation of unanswered healing prayer have you found most damaging: "God sent it", "you lacked faith", or silence? Why?

2. How can a church teach boldly on hindrances to healing without wounding the sick among them?

3. Why does God not simply override ignorance and unbelief? What does His restraint tell us about how He works?

4. What is the difference between examining yourself honestly and morbid introspection?

5. How did Jesus manage the atmosphere around miracles (Mark 5:40)? What can we copy?


### Application

Hindrances are removed, not lamented. Consider each area as debris-clearing around a finished provision.


- **Personal life.** Feed on the Gospel healing narratives this month, one story a day, noting Jesus' willingness in each. Write "I am willing" (Mark 1:41) somewhere you will see it. Knowledge is the first hindrance to fall.

- **Church community.** Change how your group responds to sickness: pray the prayer of faith rather than only offering sympathy, and never send a struggling member away with a verdict on their faith. Carry one another toward healing the way four friends carried the paralytic.

- **Difficult seasons.** If you are the one waiting, do three things steadily: keep taking the Word like medicine, repent quickly of anything real and refuse vague guilt, and surround yourself with faith-filled people. Then stand; delay is not denial, and God's will for you was published at the cross.

# Lesson 09: Forgiving Others

*Lesson 9 of 16  |  Section C: Healing and Relationships*


### Opening Question

**Whose name came to mind just now, before you even started reading? What happens inside you when you hear that name?**


### Key Scripture

**Matthew 18:21–22**
> *Then Peter came to Him and said, "Lord, how often shall my brother sin against me, and I forgive him? Up to seven times?" Jesus said to him, "I do not say to you, up to seven times, but up to seventy times seven."*

*Peter thought seven was generous; the rabbis taught three. Jesus' answer is not arithmetic but a way of life: forgiveness that never runs out of counting.*


### Core Truth

**Forgiven people forgive; the debt others owe you is pocket change beside the debt God cancelled for you, and refusing to release it hands you to the tormentors.**


### Bible Teaching


Forgiveness is not an occasional heroic act for extreme cases; in the kingdom it is the standing posture of everyone who has been forgiven. Jesus made the point with a parable so sharp it still draws blood.


#### Seventy times seven


Jesus answered Peter with a number that cannot be tracked. By the time anyone has forgiven four hundred and ninety times they have stopped counting, which is the point: forgiveness should be continual, an attitude rather than a tally. Jesus lived it, "Father, forgive them, for they do not know what they do" (Luke 23:34), and Stephen died praying it: "Lord, do not charge them with this sin" (Acts 7:60).


#### The unforgiving servant


**Matthew 18:32–33**
> *Then his master, after he had called him, said to him, "You wicked servant! I forgave you all that debt because you begged me. Should you not also have had compassion on your fellow servant, just as I had pity on you?"*


The parable (Matthew 18:23–34) turns on two absurdly mismatched debts. A servant owes his king ten thousand talents, millions upon millions, an unpayable sum, and is forgiven everything out of sheer compassion. He then finds a colleague who owes him a hundred denarii, roughly three months' wages, seizes him by the throat and has him imprisoned. The audience is meant to gasp, and then to recognise themselves.


> **Note:** The debts are the theology. Our debt to God was death itself, "the wages of sin is death" (Romans 6:23), and it was paid in full by the gift of God's Son. Every offence against us is real, sometimes grievously real, yet it stands next to our cancelled debt like pocket change beside a national debt.


#### Delivered to the tormentors


The parable ends darkly: the master delivers the unforgiving servant "to the torturers until he should pay all that was due to him", and Jesus presses it home: "So My heavenly Father also will do to you if each of you, from his heart, does not forgive his brother his trespasses" (Matthew 18:35). Unforgiveness is not a private luxury; it opens the door to torment, bitterness that corrodes, oppression that clings, sometimes sickness that will not lift, and pastors the world over can tell of freedom that came only when someone finally forgave.


> **Caution:** Forgiving is not pretending it did not hurt, excusing evil or automatically restoring trust. It is releasing the debt: handing the case and the debtor to God, and refusing to be the one who collects. Reconciliation takes two; release takes only you.


#### Forgive when you pray


**Mark 11:25**
> *And whenever you stand praying, if you have anything against anyone, forgive him, that your Father in heaven may also forgive you your trespasses.*


Jesus attaches forgiveness to the moment of prayer itself, which tells you how long unforgiveness should be allowed to live in a believer's heart: only as long as it takes to get to God and hand it over. Not nursed overnight, not curated for years. Whenever you stand praying is the deadline, and it comes several times a day.


> **Practical Tip:** Forgive as a decision, spoken aloud if needed: "Father, I release them; I cancel the debt; bless them." Feelings usually arrive after the decision, sometimes long after. You have not failed if the memory still stings; you have forgiven if you keep refusing to collect.


### Key Discovery Questions


1. Jesus set forgiveness at seventy times seven (Matthew 18:22). Who in your life is currently somewhere in that count, and where have you stalled?

2. In the parable the forgiven servant seized a man over pocket change (Matthew 18:28–30). When did an offence against you last feel enormous? How does it look beside your own cancelled debt?

3. Matthew 18:35 says the forgiveness must come "from his heart". What is the difference between forgiving from the heart and forgiving from the teeth?

4. Mark 11:25 makes prayer the deadline for releasing offence. What would obeying that verse literally change about your prayer life this week?

5. Is there any torment in your life, bitterness, heaviness, obsessive replaying, that traces back to an uncancelled debt? What would release cost you, and what is unforgiveness already costing you?


### Group Discussion Questions


1. Why do you think Jesus told a story about money to teach forgiveness? What does debt language capture that other language misses?

2. What is forgiveness not? Work through the difference between release, excusing, trusting again and reconciling.

3. Jesus and Stephen both forgave while the offence was still happening. How is that even possible?

4. Have you seen unforgiveness visibly torment someone, or forgiveness visibly free someone? What did it teach you?

5. How do we help someone forgive a truly terrible wrong without cheapening what was done to them?


### Application

Forgiveness is the most practical doctrine in this course. Consider where release needs to happen.


- **Personal life.** Write the name that came to mind at the Opening Question. Before the week ends, speak the release out loud to God: "I forgive; I cancel the debt; I hand the case to You." Repeat as often as the account tries to reopen; each repetition is obedience, not failure.

- **Church community.** Refuse to be a courier of offence. When someone brings you a grievance about a third person, help them toward Mark 11:25 rather than deepening the file. Churches split over collected debts; they heal where release is the culture.

- **Difficult seasons.** If the wound is severe, do not forgive alone: ask a mature believer to pray with you through it, and take it in instalments if needed, releasing today what you can release today. God knows the difference between a hard heart and a broken one that is still choosing to obey.

  **A:** "Father, forgive them, for they do not know what they do." Luke 23:34

  **A:** "For the wages of sin is death, but the gift of God is eternal life in Christ Jesus our Lord." Romans 6:23

# Lesson 10: Marriage (Part 1)

*Lesson 10 of 16  |  Section C: Healing and Relationships*


### Opening Question

**Where did you learn what a marriage is supposed to look like? Think of the actual examples you watched growing up. What did they teach you, for better or worse?**


### Key Scripture

**Genesis 2:24**
> *Therefore a man shall leave his father and mother and be joined to his wife, and they shall become one flesh.*

*Three movements in one verse: leave, be joined, become one. Every marriage difficulty touches at least one of the three.*


### Core Truth

**Marriage is God's good design, a man, a woman and God joined in one purpose, and it thrives not on found feelings but on learned, Christ-centred love.**


### Bible Teaching


The statistics on marriage, inside and outside the church, tell one consistent story: something widely attempted is widely misunderstood. Scripture's claim is bold: marriage was designed by God, for our joy, and it works when built to the Designer's pattern.


#### God's idea, not ours


**Genesis 2:18**
> *And the LORD God said, "It is not good that man should be alone; I will make him a helper comparable to him."*


Marriage was not invented by culture and cannot be redefined by it. God saw the one "not good" in an otherwise very good creation (Genesis 1:31), loneliness, and answered it with covenant companionship. The design brief is happiness: a helper comparable, a joining, a oneness.


> **Note:** Genesis 5:1–2 contains a striking detail: God created them male and female "and called them Mankind [Adam] in the day they were created." He named the two of them with one name. The union is not a partnership of convenience; it is one entity in God's sight.


#### Leave, cleave, become one


The verse every biblical teaching on marriage returns to is Genesis 2:24. Leaving establishes the new household's priority over every previous loyalty. Cleaving, the Hebrew idea of adhering, being glued, means a commitment that holds surfaces together under stress. Becoming one flesh is the fruit of the first two, a growing unity of purpose, body and life. And the union is a triad, not a pair: husband, wife and God together, which is why Peter warns that marital discord hinders prayer itself (1 Peter 3:7).


#### Love is learned, not found


**Titus 2:4**
> *That they admonish the young women to love their husbands, to love their children.*


If love can be taught, love is not merely a feeling that comes and goes; it is a skill and a choice. Many people enter marriage having never watched love done well, carrying dysfunction as their only pattern; the gospel says they are not doomed to repeat it. Love, God's kind, is defined by action.


**1 Corinthians 13:4**
> *Love suffers long and is kind; love does not envy; love does not parade itself, is not puffed up.*


> **Caution:** "I fell out of love" treats love as weather: something that happens to you. Scripture treats love as agriculture: something you sow, tend and grow. Feelings follow actions far more reliably than actions follow feelings; the couple who begin doing love recover the feeling of it.


#### Christ at the centre


**John 15:5**
> *I am the vine, you are the branches. He who abides in Me, and I in him, bears much fruit; for without Me you can do nothing.*


"Without Me you can do nothing" applies to marriage with special force, because marriage was designed from the beginning to picture something beyond itself: Christ and His church (Ephesians 5:31–32). A marriage aimed only at each other runs out of fuel; a marriage abiding in the vine has a source deeper than either partner's reserves. The Golden Rule (Matthew 7:12) becomes the daily operating system: do for your spouse what you wish they would do for you, before they do it.


> **Practical Tip:** Ask each other this week: what is one thing I could do daily that would make you feel loved? Then do it whether or not you feel like it. Titus 2:4 says love is learnable; every skill is learned by practice.


### Key Discovery Questions


1. Genesis 2:24 gives three movements: leave, cleave, become one. Which of the three is currently weakest in your marriage, or in your preparation for marriage?

2. God answered "it is not good that man should be alone" with marriage (Genesis 2:18). What does this tell you about what marriage is for, and what it is not for?

3. 1 Peter 3:7 says marital discord hinders prayers. Why do you think God ties the two together so tightly?

4. Titus 2:4 assumes love can be taught. What did your upbringing teach you about love, and what has God had to reteach?

5. Run 1 Corinthians 13:4 over your last week at home: where did love suffer long, and where did it parade itself?


### Group Discussion Questions


1. What models of marriage shaped you, and how do you keep the good while breaking with the bad?

2. Why does "leaving" father and mother matter so much? What does failing to leave look like in practice?

3. Is it true that feelings follow actions more than actions follow feelings? Share evidence from your own experience.

4. What does a marriage look like when Christ is genuinely at the centre rather than merely invited on Sundays?

5. How can a church community strengthen the marriages within it rather than leaving each couple to survive alone?


### Application

Marriage grows by deliberate practice of the design. Consider these areas, married or preparing.


- **Personal life.** Apply Matthew 7:12 at home for seven days as an experiment: each morning ask what you would most want from your spouse today, then do that for them first, without announcing it. Keep private notes on what changes.

- **Church community.** Couples flourish in company. Seek out a marriage further down the road than yours and learn from it over a meal; if yours is the further one, invite a younger couple. Titus 2 assumes the generations teach one another.

- **Difficult seasons.** When the marriage itself is the storm, return to the vine before you return to the argument: "without Me you can do nothing" (John 15:5). Pray together even briefly, because 1 Peter 3:7 works in reverse too: restored honour reopens heaven.

# Lesson 11: Marriage (Part 2)

*Lesson 11 of 16  |  Section C: Healing and Relationships*


### Opening Question

**If your spouse, or your future spouse, could replay everything you said about them to others this month, would the recording build them up or pull them down?**


### Key Scripture

**Ephesians 5:25**
> *Husbands, love your wives, just as Christ also loved the church and gave Himself for her.*

*The standard set here is not fairness, compatibility or fifty-fifty. It is the cross.*


### Core Truth

**Marriage is a covenant, not a contract: a binding oath of love kept by daily choices and life-giving words, mirroring Christ and His church.**


### Bible Teaching


Part 1 established the design; Part 2 goes deeper into the nature of the bond. What exactly makes a marriage a marriage? Scripture's answer, covenant, separates the biblical vision from every casual imitation.


#### Covenant, not arrangement


**Ezekiel 16:8**
> *When I passed by you again and looked upon you, indeed your time was the time of love; so I spread My wing over you and covered your nakedness. Yes, I swore an oath to you and entered into a covenant with you, and you became Mine.*


God describes His own bond with His people in marriage language: an oath, a covenant, a "you became Mine". The Hebrew word for covenant, berith, means a binding together, final until death. Physical union alone does not create marriage (1 Corinthians 6:15–16); covenant does, the sworn, exclusive, forsaking-all-others commitment that Proverbs 2:17 calls "the covenant of her God". Within that security, and only within it, the one-flesh gift is safe (Hebrews 13:4).


> **Note:** A contract protects me from you: it defines exits and penalties. A covenant binds me to you: it burns the exits. Marriages built on contract thinking renegotiate under pressure; covenant marriages absorb pressure and set like concrete.


#### Love as Christ loved


**Ephesians 5:28**
> *So husbands ought to love their own wives as their own bodies; he who loves his wife loves himself.*


Christ's love for the church is the template: He gave Himself for her. Practically that means protection, provision, nourishing and cherishing, never taking a spouse for granted, and never exposing their weaknesses to others. Isaiah diagnosed the natural condition, "we have turned, every one, to his own way" (Isaiah 53:6); covenant love turns deliberately toward the other instead.


**1 John 3:18**
> *My little children, let us not love in word or in tongue, but in deed and in truth.*


#### The power of spoken words


Ephesians 5:25–26 says Christ sanctifies the church "with the washing of water by the word", and the Greek there is rhema: spoken words. Words spoken over a spouse wash or wound; steady affirmation lifts a marriage the way steady criticism sinks one. Many spouses are starving on a diet of correction; covenant love feeds with speech.


> **Caution:** Public teasing that lands on a spouse's weakness is not humour; it is exposure, the opposite of covering (Ezekiel 16:8). Whatever the laughter in the room, something in the covenant flinches. Praise publicly, correct privately, always.


#### Love that is willed, then felt


**Galatians 5:22–23**
> *But the fruit of the Spirit is love, joy, peace, longsuffering, kindness, goodness, faithfulness, gentleness, self-control. Against such there is no law.*


Agape love is volitional: chosen daily in small deeds (John 14:15; 1 John 5:3), and it is supernatural: fruit of the Spirit, not natural human capacity strained to its limit. The same Spirit who binds Christ to His church supplies the love that binds husband to wife. And the stakes are glorious: every faithful marriage preaches without words, "this is a great mystery, but I speak concerning Christ and the church" (Ephesians 5:32). Rejoice, then, in the wife of your youth (Ecclesiastes 9:9); he who finds a wife finds a good thing (Proverbs 18:22).


> **Practical Tip:** Institute a daily word of life: one specific, true, spoken affirmation of your spouse every day, no repeats within a week. Thirty days will change the weather of the house; rhema words wash.


### Key Discovery Questions


1. Ezekiel 16:8 shows God covering, swearing an oath and saying "you became Mine". Which element of covenant, covering, oath, belonging, does your marriage (or view of marriage) most need strengthened?

2. What is the practical difference in daily behaviour between a contract mindset and a covenant mindset in marriage?

3. Ephesians 5:25 sets the cross as the standard of a husband's love. What would "gave Himself for her" look like in an ordinary Tuesday?

4. Audit your recent words about and to your spouse against Ephesians 5:25–26. Are they washing or wearing? Give real examples.

5. Galatians 5:22 makes love fruit of the Spirit. Where have you been straining for what you should be drawing from the vine?


### Group Discussion Questions


1. Why has covenant thinking become rare, and what would it take to recover it in our generation?

2. How does knowing marriage pictures Christ and the church (Ephesians 5:32) change how we treat it?

3. What is the most life-giving thing a spouse has said or could say? Why do words carry such weight in marriage?

4. Where is the line between honest humour and harmful exposure of a spouse in company?

5. How can the unmarried in the group live out covenant faithfulness now, and support the married well?


### Application

Covenants are kept in inches: daily words, daily deeds. Consider these areas.


- **Personal life.** Begin the thirty-day word of life practice: one specific spoken affirmation of your spouse daily. Add one deed of 1 John 3:18 love each week that costs you something real: time, preference or pride.

- **Church community.** Honour marriage publicly (Hebrews 13:4): celebrate anniversaries in the body, speak well of your spouse in company, and refuse to join conversations that mock husbands or wives as a class. Culture is built one comment at a time.

- **Difficult seasons.** When covenant is tested by dryness or conflict, act toward the marriage as Christ acts toward the church: keep covering, keep speaking life, keep choosing deeds of love before feelings return. Feelings are usually the last to arrive and the first to be resurrected by obedience.

# Lesson 12: God's Kind of Love (Part 1)

*Lesson 12 of 16  |  Section C: Healing and Relationships*


### Opening Question

**You have said "I love you" to people, food and football teams. When you say it to God or to another person, what do you actually mean by it?**


### Key Scripture

**1 Corinthians 13:13**
> *And now abide faith, hope, love, these three; but the greatest of these is love.*

*Of everything that survives this life, Scripture crowns one thing greatest. This lesson asks what exactly that thing is.*


### Core Truth

**God's kind of love, agape, is not a feeling that comes and goes but a chosen commitment to another's good, and it is the one thing you keep beyond the grave.**


### Bible Teaching


English is poor in love-words: one syllable covers everything from romance to pizza. Greek, the language of the New Testament, distinguishes four loves, and the difference between them is the difference between marriages that last and fail, churches that heal and split, lives that count and drift.


#### Four words for love


Eros is romantic and physical attraction, God's invention, celebrated in the Song of Solomon, and reserved by design for marriage. Storge is family affection, the natural bond of parent and child. Phileo, used over seventy times in the New Testament, is warm companionable affection, real but tidal: it rises and falls in intensity, which is why "falling in and out of love" describes phileo perfectly and why phileo alone cannot hold a covenant together. Agape is God's own love: a settled commitment to seek another's welfare regardless of feeling, cost or response.


> **Note:** Most heartbreak comes from asking one love to do another's job: expecting phileo's feelings to sustain what only agape's commitment can, or letting eros lead where agape has not yet built. The loves are good in their places; agape is the load-bearing wall.


#### Agape defined by God


**1 John 5:3**
> *For this is the love of God, that we keep His commandments. And His commandments are not burdensome.*


**Matthew 7:12**
> *Therefore, whatever you want men to do to you, do also to them, for this is the Law and the Prophets.*


Scripture defines agape not by intensity of emotion but by direction of will: keeping God's commandments, doing for others what you would want done for you. This runs against the grain of the flesh, seeking another's benefit above your own is not natural, which is precisely why love is listed as fruit of the Spirit: God supplies both the instruction and the strength.


**Romans 13:10**
> *Love does no harm to a neighbor; therefore love is the fulfillment of the law.*


#### Love your enemies, without waiting to feel it


**Romans 12:21**
> *Do not be overcome by evil, but overcome evil with good.*


Agape makes even enemy-love possible, because it never waits for feelings. Romans 12:19–21 hands vengeance to God and hands us actions: feed the hungry enemy, give the thirsty one a drink. You can seek the welfare of someone you do not like; that is not hypocrisy, it is agape doing its work while feelings catch up.


> **Going Deeper:** A ministry worker once befriended a refugee family in a park purely as a decision: money given, food shared, furniture found, years of practical kindness with no emotional glow. In time the family came to faith, a wider ministry to migrants grew from it, and warm affection flooded in behind the obedience. The pattern is reliable: practise agape and phileo grows in its shade. If you want a love that feels, do the love that acts.


#### Love in deed and in truth


**1 John 3:18**
> *My little children, let us not love in word or in tongue, but in deed and in truth.*


Talk is love's cheapest currency. The proof of agape is always enacted: kindness, patience, humility (1 Corinthians 13:4), commandments kept, welfare sought. Pursue this love as your highest aim (1 Corinthians 14:1), because when faith becomes sight and hope becomes possession, love alone goes on.


> **Practical Tip:** Choose one person this week, family, colleague or someone you find difficult, and perform three deliberate acts of agape for them: practical, costly and unannounced. Record what happens to your feelings toward them by the weekend.


### Key Discovery Questions


1. Which of the four loves, eros, storge, phileo, agape, has dominated your understanding of the word "love", and how has that shaped your relationships?

2. 1 John 5:3 defines loving God as keeping His commandments. Where has your definition been more sentimental than obedient?

3. Romans 12:20–21 commands feeding a hungry enemy. Who is the nearest thing you have to an enemy, and what would "good" toward them actually look like?

4. "Practise agape and phileo grows in its shade." Where have you experienced feelings following obedience, in either direction?

5. 1 Corinthians 13:13 says love is greater than faith and hope. Why do you think that is, and does your diary agree?


### Group Discussion Questions


1. How has English's single word "love" confused our thinking? Share examples.

2. Why is phileo, warm affection, an unstable foundation for marriage or church life on its own?

3. Is acting lovingly toward someone you dislike hypocrisy? Argue both sides, then settle it from Scripture.

4. What is the relationship between love and obedience in 1 John 5:3? Does that make love cold, or does it protect it?

5. Where have you seen agape love visibly change a person, a family or a community?


### Application

Agape is a practice with a promise: feelings grow behind it. Consider these areas.


- **Personal life.** Run the three-acts experiment: one person, three deliberate, costly, unannounced acts of agape this week. Judge the week by obedience, not by warmth; then notice the warmth anyway.

- **Church community.** Aim agape at the overlooked: the newcomer, the awkward, the one who never gets invited. 1 Corinthians 14:1 says pursue love; pursuit means moving first, without waiting to be moved.

- **Difficult seasons.** When you feel unloved or unloving, act first: do for someone what you wish were done for you (Matthew 7:12). Agape given in a desert is the fastest way out of one, because the fruit of the Spirit grows in every soil.

# Lesson 13: God's Kind of Love (Part 2)

*Lesson 13 of 16  |  Section C: Healing and Relationships*


### Opening Question

**Think of the person who has most convinced you they love you. How much of that conviction came from what they said, and how much from what they did?**


### Key Scripture

**Matthew 25:40**
> *And the King will answer and say to them, "Assuredly, I say to you, inasmuch as you did it to one of the least of these My brethren, you did it to Me."*

*Every act of practical love toward the least has a second recipient. Read the verse until you see who.*


### Core Truth

**Love is something you do: nearly all of it is nonverbal, the least of these are its address, and Jesus Himself receives every deed of it.**


### Bible Teaching


Here is a striking fact: the Gospels never record Jesus saying the words "I love you". The greatest lover in history demonstrated rather than declared, feeding, touching, healing, washing feet, dying. This lesson presses one truth home: love is overwhelmingly something you do.


#### Ninety-five per cent nonverbal


**Matthew 25:35–36**
> *For I was hungry and you gave Me food; I was thirsty and you gave Me drink; I was a stranger and you took Me in; I was naked and you clothed Me; I was sick and you visited Me; I was in prison and you came to Me.*


When Jesus describes love at the final judgement, every item is a deed: food, drink, welcome, clothing, visits. Words barely feature. If your words say love and your actions say otherwise, everyone, including you, believes the actions.


> **Note:** God's own love was demonstrated, not merely declared: "But God demonstrates His own love toward us, in that while we were still sinners, Christ died for us" (Romans 5:8). The cross is love's grammar: a verb.


#### The second recipient


Matthew 25:40 reveals the staggering economics of kindness: whatever is done for the least is received by the King. You cannot make Jesus a meal, visit Him in hospital or clothe Him this week, except that you can, at the address of the nearest person in need. "For God is not unjust to forget your work and labor of love which you have shown toward His name, in that you have ministered to the saints" (Hebrews 6:10).


> **Going Deeper:** This truth abolishes the idea that ministry to people and devotion to God compete. In Scripture they are one motion: love God, love neighbour, the two commandments on which everything hangs (Matthew 22:37–40). The soup you carry across the road goes to two doors at once.


#### Love does not wait to feel like it


Did Jesus feel like dying? Gethsemane answers: "not My will, but Yours". Love chose our welfare against overwhelming feeling. That settles a question many believers never resolve: love is not hostage to mood. It seeks the other's benefit regardless, and this is only possible with God at the centre, "for God is love" (1 John 4:8); cut off from the source, the stream dries.


**1 Corinthians 13:8**
> *Love never fails. But whether there are prophecies, they will fail; whether there are tongues, they will cease; whether there is knowledge, it will vanish away.*


#### What love covers and carries


**Proverbs 10:12**
> *Hatred stirs up strife, But love covers all sins.*


Love is also what remains: prophecies complete their work, knowledge is superseded, but love never fails, and love is the one treasure carried beyond the grave. A life spent on deeds of agape is the only portfolio that survives the exchange rate of eternity. So become the right person rather than hunting for the right person; do the Golden Rule (Matthew 7:12) before it is done to you; and let love cover what hatred would publish.


> **Practical Tip:** This week, love Jesus directly through Matthew 25: pick one, feed, refresh, welcome, clothe or visit, and do it for a "least" who can never repay you. Tell no one except Him.


### Key Discovery Questions

### Group Discussion Questions

2. Why do words of love ring hollow without deeds, while deeds of love speak even without words?

3. Does Matthew 25 teach salvation by works? Work out together what it does and does not say.

4. Can we truly love without God at the centre (1 John 4:8)? What does merely human love run out of?

5. What is the one thing you take beyond the grave, and how should that reorder a diary and a bank account?


### Application

Love's address book is full of the least. Consider where the deeds should land.


- **Personal life.** Choose one Matthew 25 deed this week, done secretly for someone who cannot repay. Let Hebrews 6:10 be the only receipt: God is not unjust to forget.

- **Church community.** Audit your fellowship's love by deeds: who is hungry, isolated, unvisited or struggling within your reach? Organise the smallest possible response this month, two people and a meal beats a committee and a plan.

- **Difficult seasons.** When you feel forgotten, love someone anyway. Ministering to the least when you are the least is the most Christ-like act available, and it is often the door out of the pit; His love flows fastest through empty hands.

# Lesson 14: Finances (Part 1)

*Lesson 14 of 16  |  Section D: Provision and Prayer*


### Opening Question

**Complete this sentence honestly: "Deep down, I believe God's attitude to me having money is…" Where did that belief come from?**


### Key Scripture

**2 Corinthians 9:8**
> *And God is able to make all grace abound toward you, that you, always having all sufficiency in all things, may have an abundance for every good work.*

*Count the sweep of the verse: all grace, always, all sufficiency, all things, abundance, every good work. Then notice what the abundance is for.*


### Core Truth

**God cares about your provision, spirit, soul and body, and biblical prosperity is having all sufficiency plus an abundance, not to hoard but for every good work.**


### Bible Teaching


Money is one of the most discipled areas of life or one of the least, never neither. Religious tradition has often taught that poverty is next to godliness; Scripture teaches something more balanced and more demanding: God provides generously, and He does it for a purpose.


#### God's attitude to your provision


**3 John 2**
> *Beloved, I pray that you may prosper in all things and be in health, just as your soul prospers.*


John's prayer for a beloved believer covers prospering, health and soul in one breath; God's concern is the whole person. The Scriptures are full of God's people prospering in His service: Abraham was very rich (Genesis 13:2), and David gave from his own treasure a sum in gold and silver that would be measured in billions today for the building of the temple. Wealth in consecrated hands has always funded the purposes of God.


#### The love of money, not money


**1 Timothy 6:10**
> *For the love of money is a root of all kinds of evil, for which some have strayed from the faith in their greediness, and pierced themselves through with many sorrows.*


Scripture's warning is precise: not money but the love of it, not possession but greed. The rich are commanded not to trust riches but to be "rich in good works, ready to give, willing to share" (1 Timothy 6:17–18). Money is a tool; it makes a superb servant and a murderous master.


> **Caution:** Two ditches line this road. One says God wants everyone poor, and calls the lack "holiness". The other says God exists to make you rich, and calls the greed "faith". Scripture walks between them: provision with purpose, abundance aimed at good works.


#### Why God prospers people


**Deuteronomy 8:18**
> *And you shall remember the LORD your God, for it is He who gives you power to get wealth, that He may establish His covenant which He swore to your fathers, as it is this day.*


**Genesis 12:2**
> *I will make you a great nation; I will bless you And make your name great; And you shall be a blessing.*


The purpose clause matters in both verses: power to get wealth is given to establish God's covenant, and Abraham is blessed to be a blessing. The blessing cycle runs bless, then be a blessing; provision flows to you so it can flow through you. Ephesians 4:28 even gives work this aim: labour "that he may have something to give him who has need".


> **Note:** 2 Corinthians 9:8 is the Bible's definition of prosperity: all sufficiency in all things, needs met, plus an abundance for every good work, surplus with an assignment. By that definition a believer on a modest income who gives freely is prosperous, and a millionaire who cannot release a pound is poor.


#### Seek first the kingdom


**Matthew 6:33**
> *But seek first the kingdom of God and His righteousness, and all these things shall be added to you.*


The order is everything. Money makes a terrible god and a fine servant; seek the kingdom first and provision is added, seek provision first and both are lost. Motive is the watershed: asking for more to consume on self is greed dressed in prayer, asking for more to bless and build is covenant partnership.


> **Practical Tip:** Write a "generosity wish list": the good works you would fund if you had abundance. Pray over it weekly. It aims your heart before God aims His supply, and it makes Matthew 6:33 measurable in your own diary.


### Key Discovery Questions


1. What did your upbringing and church background teach you, in words or in silence, about God and money? Test it against 3 John 2.

2. 1 Timothy 6:10 blames the love of money, not money. Which do you actually battle: the love of having it, or fear about lacking it? What is the difference?

3. Deuteronomy 8:18 says God gives power to get wealth to establish His covenant. How would your working life change if you saw your earning power that way?

4. By 2 Corinthians 9:8's definition, sufficiency plus abundance for good works, are you currently prosperous? Explain your answer.

5. Genesis 12:2 runs "blessed to be a blessing". Where has the flow stopped with you rather than through you?


### Group Discussion Questions


1. Why has the church so often swung between poverty-as-holiness and prosperity-as-entitlement? What does each get wrong?

2. Can a believer be wealthy and godly? Can a believer be poor and godly? What do Abraham, David and Jesus each contribute to the answer?

3. What does the love of money look like in someone who has very little?

4. How do we test our motives when praying about finances?

5. What good works in our community are waiting on someone's abundance? Could it be ours?


### Application

Provision follows purpose. Consider how a blessed-to-be-a-blessing life looks in each area.


- **Personal life.** Draft your generosity wish list this week and pray over it. Then memorise Matthew 6:33 and let it audit one financial decision this month: kingdom first, addition second.

- **Church community.** Practise 1 Timothy 6:18 corporately: be ready to give, willing to share. Find one practical need in the body, a bill, a repair, a shortfall, and meet it quietly with others.

- **Difficult seasons.** In lack, refuse both panic and shame. Bring the need to the God of 2 Corinthians 9:8, keep sowing what you can from what you have, and remember that His record with His people in famine is remarkable reading.

- **Q:** What is God able to make abound toward you? (2 Corinthians 9:8)  
  **A:** "And God is able to make all grace abound toward you, that you, always having all sufficiency in all things, may have an abundance for every good work." 2 Corinthians 9:8

- **Q:** What did John pray for the beloved? (3 John 2)  
  **A:** "Beloved, I pray that you may prosper in all things and be in health, just as your soul prospers." 3 John 2

  **A:** "For the love of money is a root of all kinds of evil." 1 Timothy 6:10

  **A:** "For it is He who gives you power to get wealth, that He may establish His covenant which He swore to your fathers." Deuteronomy 8:18

- **Q:** What must be sought first? (Matthew 6:33)  
  **A:** "But seek first the kingdom of God and His righteousness, and all these things shall be added to you." Matthew 6:33

  **A:** All sufficiency in all things plus an abundance for every good work (2 Corinthians 9:8): needs met and surplus with an assignment. Blessed in order to be a blessing.

# Lesson 15: Finances (Part 2)

*Lesson 15 of 16  |  Section D: Provision and Prayer*


### Opening Question

**When did giving last cost you something you actually felt? What went through your mind as you let it go?**


### Key Scripture

**Luke 6:38**
> *Give, and it will be given to you: good measure, pressed down, shaken together, and running over will be put into your bosom. For with the same measure that you use, it will be measured back to you.*

*The verse begins with a command of one word and spends the rest describing the return. Note whose measure sets the size.*


### Core Truth

**Giving is the engine of biblical finance: measured by proportion not amount, powered by love not obligation, and trusted to a God who repays what is lent to Him.**


### Bible Teaching


Part 1 established God's attitude to provision; Part 2 turns to the believer's side of the covenant: giving. Scripture treats giving not as fundraising but as the central discipline by which money is mastered and faith is exercised.


#### Everyone can give


**Mark 12:43–44**
> *So He called His disciples to Himself and said to them, "Assuredly, I say to you that this poor widow has put in more than all those who have given to the treasury; for they all put in out of their abundance, but she out of her poverty put in all that she had, her whole livelihood."*


Jesus watched the offering and did the mathematics of heaven: two mites outweighed fortunes, because God measures gifts by proportion and sacrifice, not by amount. "I cannot afford to give" is answered forever by a widow; the question is never capacity but heart.


> **Note:** God's own giving sets the pattern: He gave "according to His riches" and out of love, "For God so loved the world that He gave His only begotten Son" (John 3:16; Ephesians 1:7). Giving that is not motivated by love profits nothing, though it gave all (1 Corinthians 13:3); love is the only currency heaven recognises behind the gift.


#### The paradox of generosity


**Proverbs 11:24–25**
> *There is one who scatters, yet increases more; And there is one who withholds more than is right, But it leads to poverty. The generous soul will be made rich, And he who waters will also be watered himself.*


The world's arithmetic says keeping increases and giving depletes; the kingdom's arithmetic runs the other way. Scattering increases; withholding impoverishes. Luke 6:38 attaches the return to your own measure, and Proverbs 19:17 makes the boldest claim in the book: "He who has pity on the poor lends to the LORD, And He will pay back what he has given." Giving to the poor is lending to a Borrower who has never defaulted.


> **Caution:** This is not a slot machine. Giving to get is not giving; it is shopping. The promises of return are real, but they are made to cheerful, love-driven givers, "not grudgingly or of necessity; for God loves a cheerful giver" (2 Corinthians 9:7), and empty words to the needy prove faith dead (James 2:15–16).


#### Faithful in the least


**Luke 16:10–11**
> *He who is faithful in what is least is faithful also in much; and he who is unjust in what is least is unjust also in much. Therefore if you have not been faithful in the unrighteous mammon, who will commit to your trust the true riches?*


Jesus calls money "what is least", the training ground of stewardship. How you handle pounds determines what God can trust you with in true riches: people, ministry, spiritual responsibility. Faithfulness in the small unlocks the great; there is no promotion past a failed apprenticeship in mammon.


#### Blessings on the open hand


Psalm 41:1–3 pronounces blessing on the one who considers the poor: deliverance in trouble, protection, preservation, blessing on the earth and strength on the bed of illness. God notices open hands and stands behind them. The giving life is the securest financial position on earth, because its guarantor cannot fail.


> **Practical Tip:** Set a proportion, decide it in your heart before God (2 Corinthians 9:7), and give it first when income arrives, not last from what survives the month. Firstness is what makes giving worship instead of leftovers.


### Key Discovery Questions


1. Jesus measured the widow's two mites as more than all the rest (Mark 12:43–44). What does your current giving look like when measured by proportion and sacrifice rather than amount?

2. Proverbs 11:24 describes scattering that increases and withholding that impoverishes. Where have you seen each half of the verse proven in real life?

3. Luke 16:10–11 makes money the test for true riches. What might God be waiting to entrust to you on the far side of faithfulness in the least?

4. 2 Corinthians 9:7 rules out grudging and pressured giving. What would need to change for your giving to become genuinely cheerful?

5. Proverbs 19:17 says giving to the poor is lending to the Lord. Do you have any such "loans" outstanding? What is stopping the next one?


### Group Discussion Questions


1. Why do you think Jesus watched the offering box (Mark 12:41)? What does His interest in giving tell us?

2. How do we hold the real promises of return (Luke 6:38) without sliding into giving-to-get?

3. What makes giving hard: fear, habit, past manipulation by churches, genuine lack? Be honest about the group's barriers.

4. What is the difference between the widow's recklessness and irresponsibility? How does faith tell them apart?

5. If money is the "least" (Luke 16:10), why does it feel like the most? What does that feeling reveal?


### Application

Giving is learned by giving. Consider these areas of practice.


- **Personal life.** Decide your proportion before God this week and automate its firstness: it leaves when income arrives. Review it yearly with one question: is my measure growing with my trust?

- **Church community.** Support the work of the gospel and the needy within reach: those who preach the gospel should live from the gospel (1 Corinthians 9:14), and no member of the body should face a genuine need alone while the rest have surplus.

- **Difficult seasons.** When money is tightest, do not stop giving; shrink the amount, never the habit. The widow gave in her poverty, and the God who watched her watches you. Psalm 41:1–3 was written for the giver's hardest bed.

# Lesson 16: When Prayers Seem Unanswered

*Lesson 16 of 16  |  Section D: Provision and Prayer*


### Opening Question

**What is the prayer you have almost stopped praying? What conclusion has the delay been quietly teaching you about God?**


### Key Scripture

**1 John 5:14–15**
> *Now this is the confidence that we have in Him, that if we ask anything according to His will, He hears us. And if we know that He hears us, whatever we ask, we know that we have the petitions that we have asked of Him.*

*Twice the verse says "we know". Confidence in prayer is not presumption; it is the stated inheritance of those who ask according to His will.*


### Core Truth

**God answers prayer offered in faith according to His Word; when answers tarry, the delay is in the delivery, never in His willingness to hear.**


### Bible Teaching


Every believer has prayed prayers that seemed to vanish into the ceiling. Notice the lesson's title says seem: Scripture insists that prayers according to God's will are heard and answered, and then explains, with unusual candour, why the manifestation is sometimes slow. The explanation changes how you wait.


#### The promise is unqualified


**Matthew 7:7–8**
> *Ask, and it will be given to you; seek, and you will find; knock, and it will be opened to you. For everyone who asks receives, and he who seeks finds, and to him who knocks it will be opened.*


Everyone who asks receives; the words could not be plainer, and Jesus never retracted them. God is Spirit (John 4:24); He answers in the spiritual realm, and faith is the receiver that brings the answer through into the visible: "Now faith is the substance of things hoped for, the evidence of things not seen" (Hebrews 11:1). A radio studio is full of music a dead receiver never plays; the broadcast is not the problem.


#### Daniel's two answers


**Daniel 10:12–13**
> *Then he said to me, "Do not fear, Daniel, for from the first day that you set your heart to understand, and to humble yourself before your God, your words were heard; and I have come because of your words. But the prince of the kingdom of Persia withstood me twenty-one days; and behold, Michael, one of the chief princes, came to help me."*


Twice Daniel prayed. Once the answer arrived while he was still speaking (Daniel 9:22–23); once it took three weeks. Same God, same Daniel, same hearing "from the first day". The difference was opposition in transit: a demonic prince withstood the messenger for twenty-one days. God had answered instantly both times; the second answer was delayed in delivery, and Daniel's persistence in prayer covered the gap.


> **Note:** God does not change between your fast answers and your slow ones: "Jesus Christ is the same yesterday, today, and forever" (Hebrews 13:8). The variables are elsewhere: opposition, people, and the state of the asking heart. Fixing the diagnosis on God is the one error that kills prayer altogether.


#### Answers travel through people


Many answers must come through human hands: provision especially, "give, and it will be given to you… will be put into your bosom" (Luke 6:38) describes people giving. People can be slow, reluctant or interfered with, which is another reason manifestation lags decree. Keep praying; prayer moves the hands the answer travels through.


#### Check the asking heart


**James 4:3**
> *You ask and do not receive, because you ask amiss, that you may spend it on your pleasures.*


Scripture names honest blockages on the asker's side: motives bent toward self-consumption (James 4:1–3), a mistreated spouse hindering prayers (1 Peter 3:7), and asking outside God's revealed will (1 John 5:14). The remedy for each is simple and immediate: adjust the motive, honour the spouse, align the request with the Word, and pray on with clean hands.


#### Believe you receive


**Mark 11:24**
> *Therefore I say to you, whatever things you ask when you pray, believe that you receive them, and you will have them.*


The tense of faith is present: believe you receive when you pray, and you will have. Receiving happens at the asking; having follows. This is the posture that carries a believer through any Daniel-gap: the request granted, the delivery contested, the heart already thanking. Prayers do not go unanswered; they go opposed, misrouted or mis-asked, and every one of those has a remedy that keeps you praying.


> **Practical Tip:** For your longest-standing prayer, do three things this week: check it against the Word, check the motive, then thank God daily that it is granted and in transit. Thanksgiving is what faith sounds like in the gap.


### Key Discovery Questions


1. 1 John 5:14–15 grounds confidence in asking "according to His will". For your biggest current prayer, what Scripture tells you it is His will? If none comes to mind, what is your next step?

2. Daniel's answer was dispatched on day one and arrived on day twenty-one (Daniel 10:12–13). How does knowing about the gap change how you pray during one?

3. James 4:3 names asking amiss. Take one stalled prayer and examine the motive honestly: who is the answer really for?

4. 1 Peter 3:7 links home relationships to hindered prayers. Is there anything relational you need to put right before you pray on?

5. Mark 11:24 says believe you receive when you pray. What would daily thanksgiving in the gap sound like for your longest wait?


### Group Discussion Questions


1. What conclusions do people commonly draw from delayed answers, about God, themselves or prayer? Which have you drawn?

2. How does the radio-receiver picture of faith (Hebrews 11:1) help, and where does the analogy stop?

3. Daniel prayed on through twenty-one silent days. What keeps persistence alive when nothing visibly changes?

4. Why do you think God routes so many answers through people? What does that design achieve?

5. How do we examine motives (James 4:3) without falling into the morbid introspection warned against earlier in this course?


### Application

The gap between granted and manifest is where faith does its finest work. Consider these areas.


- **Personal life.** Take your longest-standing prayer through the three checks: Word, motive, relationships. Then move it from pleading to thanksgiving: daily, specific thanks that it is granted and in transit (Mark 11:24). Memorise 1 John 5:14–15 as your confidence.

- **Church community.** Keep a group answer-book: record requests and dates, record manifestations and dates. Nothing builds corporate faith like documented Daniel-gaps closed, and nothing sustains a waiting member like a group still standing with them at day twenty.

- **Difficult seasons.** In the longest waits, guard two things: your picture of God, He is the same yesterday, today and forever (Hebrews 13:8), and your persistence, the answer may be contested precisely because it matters. Do not build a doctrine out of a delay; build an altar in it.
