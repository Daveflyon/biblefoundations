Part 2 of 3: Growing in Christ

Hindrances to Healing

Lesson 8 of 16  |  Section C: Healing and Relationships

Opening Question

Please begin by answering this question honestly in your own words.

Have you ever prayed for healing that did not come? What conclusion did you quietly draw about God, about yourself, or about prayer?

Key Scripture

Hosea 4:6

My people are destroyed for lack of knowledge. Because you have rejected knowledge, I also will reject you from being priest for Me; Because you have forgotten the law of your God, I also will forget your children.

The destruction described here is not caused by God's unwillingness but by His people's ignorance. That distinction carries the whole lesson.

Core Truth

The central idea of this lesson

God's willingness to heal is settled at the cross; where healing is hindered, the blockage lies in ignorance, sin's inroads or unbelief, never in God's reluctance.

If healing is in the atonement, why is not everyone healed? The honest question deserves an honest biblical answer, and the answer Scripture gives is liberating: the variable is never God's willingness. Several identifiable things can hinder, and every one of them can be addressed.

Ignorance

A person can perish under a law of gravity they never heard of; ignorance exempts no one from consequences. Likewise, believers who do not know that healing is provided cannot receive what they do not know exists. Whole generations have died early not because God withheld but because knowledge was lacking, and Hosea names that tragedy precisely: destroyed for lack of knowledge.

Note

This truth removes blame from God without dumping it on the sufferer. A person who prayed in the dark and lost someone did not fail; they simply had not been taught. The response to new knowledge is never guilt about the past but faith for the present.

Sin's inroads

John 9:2–3

And His disciples asked Him, saying, "Rabbi, who sinned, this man or his parents, that he was born blind?" Jesus answered, "Neither this man nor his parents sinned, but that the works of God should be revealed in him."

Scripture holds two truths in balance. Not all sickness is caused by personal sin; Jesus flatly refused that equation. Yet sin can open a door: after healing one man, Jesus warned, "See, you have been made well. Sin no more, lest a worse thing come upon you" (John 5:14). Yielding to sin yields ground (Romans 6:16), and the thief uses every inch he is given (John 10:10).

Caution

Beware morbid introspection. Some believers turn every symptom into a hunt for hidden guilt and end up believing they are too unworthy to be healed, which is itself a lie. Where there is real sin, repent and move on; the blood cleanses. Endless self-examination is not humility, it is unbelief wearing a religious coat.

Unbelief, ours and others'

Mark 6:5–6

Now He could do no mighty work there, except that He laid His hands on a few sick people and healed them. And He marveled because of their unbelief. Then He went about the villages in a circuit, teaching.

In Nazareth the limiting factor was not the Healer but the atmosphere. Matthew records that He did not do many mighty works there because of their unbelief (Matthew 13:58). Corporate scepticism can smother what God is willing to do, and He will not force healing on those who refuse to receive it; He honours the freedom He gave.

What to do

James 5:15

And the prayer of faith will save the sick, and the Lord will raise him up. And if he has committed sins, he will be forgiven.

The path through hindrances is practical: get knowledge, feeding on what Scripture actually says about healing until expectation rises; close sin's doors by simple repentance, and receive forgiveness with the healing; and guard the atmosphere, choosing who prays with you as carefully as Jesus did when He put the wailers out before raising the girl (Mark 5:40). None of these steps earns healing; they remove the debris from a provision already made.

Practical Tip

Build your faith before the crisis, not during it. Read the healing narratives of the Gospels regularly, noting each time Jesus' willingness. Knowledge stored in health is faith available in sickness.

Question My Answer Group Discussion Notes
Hosea 4:6 says God's people are destroyed for lack of knowledge. What did you not know about healing five years ago that you know now, and what difference has it made?
Jesus refused the equation "sickness equals personal sin" (John 9:3) yet warned a healed man to sin no more (John 5:14). How do you hold those two texts together?
Where does morbid introspection show up in your praying? What would simple repentance followed by confidence look like instead?
In Nazareth unbelief limited what Jesus did (Mark 6:5–6). What is the faith atmosphere of your home and church, and what feeds it?
James 5:15 joins healing and forgiveness in one prayer. Why do you think God packages them together?

These questions are designed for open conversation at any level of experience. There are no trick questions and no single correct answer.

  1. Which explanation of unanswered healing prayer have you found most damaging: "God sent it", "you lacked faith", or silence? Why?
  2. How can a church teach boldly on hindrances to healing without wounding the sick among them?
  3. Why does God not simply override ignorance and unbelief? What does His restraint tell us about how He works?
  4. What is the difference between examining yourself honestly and morbid introspection?
  5. How did Jesus manage the atmosphere around miracles (Mark 5:40)? What can we copy?

Hindrances are removed, not lamented. Consider each area as debris-clearing around a finished provision.

Context How I Apply This
Personal life Feed on the Gospel healing narratives this month, one story a day, noting Jesus' willingness in each. Write "I am willing" (Mark 1:41) somewhere you will see it. Knowledge is the first hindrance to fall.
Church community Change how your group responds to sickness: pray the prayer of faith rather than only offering sympathy, and never send a struggling member away with a verdict on their faith. Carry one another toward healing the way four friends carried the paralytic.
Difficult seasons If you are the one waiting, do three things steadily: keep taking the Word like medicine, repent quickly of anything real and refuse vague guilt, and surround yourself with faith-filled people. Then stand; delay is not denial, and God's will for you was published at the cross.
