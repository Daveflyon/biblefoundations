Part 3 of 3: Serving in Christ

Using Everyone's Gifts to Disciple

Lesson 16 of 16  |  Section D: Living as a Disciple

Opening Question

Please begin by answering this question honestly in your own words.

If every believer in your church used their specific gifts for discipleship and outreach, not just the pastors, what do you think would actually change?

Key Scripture

Ephesians 4:12

For the equipping of the saints for the work of ministry, for the edifying of the body of Christ.

Read this slowly before moving on. Notice who does the work of ministry here: the saints, not only their leaders.

Core Truth

The central idea of this lesson

Discipleship and evangelism were never meant to rest on a gifted few; every believer’s specific gift has a role to play in building up the church and reaching others.

A striking gap

A striking pattern is visible in the modern church: the vast majority of believers never personally evangelise, leaving outreach to a small minority, often clergy alone.

Leaders equip; the saints minister

Ephesians 4:11-12

And He Himself gave some to be apostles, some prophets, some evangelists, and some pastors and teachers, for the equipping of the saints for the work of ministry, for the edifying of the body of Christ.

The five-fold ministry gifts exist specifically to equip the saints, ordinary believers, for ministry, not to perform all the ministry themselves.

Doers, not only hearers

James 1:22

But be doers of the word, and not hearers only, deceiving yourselves.

James ties genuine faith to active participation, not passive attendance.

Every gift necessary

1 Corinthians 12:21

And the eye cannot say to the hand, "I have no need of you"; nor again the head to the feet, "I have no need of you."

The body of Christ, by design, is interdependent. Team-based ministry, using teachers, helpers, intercessors and practical servers together, mobilises far more of the body than reliance on a few visible gifts alone.

The full arc, gathered together

This lesson closes the series by gathering its whole arc into one picture: a body empowered by the Spirit, joined to one another in real community and tested by real opposition, standing free from guilt and fully accepted in Christ, and now sent out, every member equipped and participating, to disciple the world Christ has commissioned it to reach.

Acts 1:8

But you shall receive power when the Holy Spirit has come upon you; and you shall be witnesses to Me in Jerusalem, and in all Judea and Samaria, and to the end of the earth.

Going Deeper

The statistic that a large majority of Christians never personally evangelise is not offered as a condemnation but as a diagnosis: it names precisely the gap this final lesson, and this entire Part, exists to close. Every believer, equipped by the Spirit, gifted specifically, free from guilt, and bearing visible fruit, already has what is needed to disciple someone else using their own story and their own gift.

Question My Answer Group Discussion Notes
What specific gift do you believe God has given you that could be used to disciple or reach someone else?
James 1:22 calls believers to be "doers," not just "hearers." Where in your discipleship life are you currently more of a hearer than a doer?
1 Corinthians 12:21 says no gift can say to another "I have no need of you." Which gift in your church community do you tend to undervalue?
Looking back across all of Part 3, from the Spirit’s power to this final lesson, what single truth has most changed how you see your role in the church?

These questions are designed for open conversation at any level of experience. There are no trick questions and no single correct answer.

  1. Why do you think such a small percentage of believers ever personally evangelise, even in healthy churches?
  2. Share one practical, quieter gift (hospitality, administration, practical help) you have seen used powerfully for discipleship or outreach.
  3. What would it look like for your own church community to become less "inward-focused" and more oriented toward reaching beyond itself?
  4. Looking back over Section D as a whole, fruit, discipleship, testimony, and now everyone’s gifts, what is one concrete step you will take as a result?

This is the final lesson of the whole series. Let it move from reflection into one concrete act of ministry this week.
