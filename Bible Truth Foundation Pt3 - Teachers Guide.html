Teacher’s Guide

Bible Truth Foundations – Part 3 of 3: Serving in Christ

The Well Shrewsbury

Part A: How to Use This Guide

This guide accompanies the sixteen student lessons of Part 3 and is for facilitators only; it is never distributed with the student files. Each lesson entry below gives the facilitator’s aim, the key scripture and core truth as they appear in the student material, specific things to watch for in the room, and a pastoral note. The student lesson carries the full teaching text; your role is not to re-teach it but to steward the discussion it opens.

Suggested session shape (75–90 minutes): open with the Opening Question cold, before any teaching (10 minutes); read the Key Scripture aloud and let the group sit with it (5); walk the Bible Teaching section together, expanding where the room needs it (25–30); Group Discussion Questions (20); Application and prayer (15). Key Discovery Questions are completed by members at home before the session; begin discussion by asking what they found.

All scripture in the series is New King James Version, declared once at series level. British English throughout. Lessons build in order; resist requests to jump ahead, especially into Section C (Lessons 7–11), which depends on the freedom-in-Christ foundation of Sections A and B.

Part B: Per-Lesson Guide

Section A — Living in the Spirit's Power

Lesson 01: The Divine Flow

Facilitator’s aim: Move the group from admiring compassion in the abstract to recognising and releasing it as something already given, tested against 1 Corinthians 13.

Key Scripture: Philemon 1:6 — That the sharing of your faith may become effective by the acknowledgment of every good thing which is in you in Christ Jesus.

Core Truth

God’s love is not something you work up. It is something already placed in you by the Holy Spirit, waiting to be acknowledged and released.

Watch for:

  • Some will hear “already in you” as licence to skip discernment. Stress the 1 Corinthians 13 test; not every strong feeling is the divine flow.
  • The gap between feeling compassion and acting on it is where most people live. Give real space to name what stopped them, not just what moved them.
  • Guard against turning this into a session about spiritual gifts generally; that is Lesson 2's territory. Keep this one on recognising and releasing love specifically.

Pastoral note

Expect someone to disclose a prompting they regret not acting on. Do not let guilt take over; redirect toward the next opportunity, since the flow is available again, not a one-time chance missed forever.

Lesson 02: Using the Gifts to Minister

Facilitator’s aim: Help every member identify at least one gift they already carry and take one small step toward using it this week.

Key Scripture: 1 Peter 4:11 — If anyone speaks, let him speak as the oracles of God. If anyone ministers, let him do it as with the ability which God supplies, that in all things God may be glorified through Jesus Christ.

Core Truth

Every believer has been given a specific gift by the Holy Spirit, and ministry is meant to flow through God’s supplied ability, not your own natural talent.

Watch for:

  • Members will default to naming only the dramatic gifts (healing, tongues). Deliberately draw out the Romans 12 list: service, giving, mercy, leading.
  • Some will feel disqualified because they cannot point to a gift. Slow down on 1 Corinthians 12:11; the Spirit gives to each one, without exception.
  • Keep the “ability God supplies” distinction concrete; ask for an example of leaning on God’s strength rather than natural skill in the room, not just in theory.

Pastoral note

This lesson often produces quiet self-comparison, someone measuring their gift against a more visible one. Watch for it and affirm quieter gifts by name before the session ends.

Lesson 03: Miracles Glorify God

Facilitator’s aim: Anchor miracles to their true purpose, glorifying God and confirming His Word, so the group neither chases signs nor doubts God still works in power.

Key Scripture: Matthew 9:8 — Now when the multitudes saw it, they marvelled and glorified God, who had given such power to men.

Core Truth

Miracles are never given for the minister’s credit; they exist to point people to God and confirm His Word as true.

Watch for:

  • Scepticism and hunger for spectacle often sit in the same room. Let both speak; the lesson's caution applies to both extremes.
  • The “dinner bell” image lands well; use it, but do not let the group treat miracles as marketing rather than genuine confirmation of the Gospel.
  • If someone shares a claimed miracle, resist the urge to verify or debunk it in the room. Receive it, then return to the lesson's actual point: where does the glory go?

Pastoral note

Some in the group may carry disappointment over an unanswered prayer for healing or a miracle. This lesson is not the place to resolve that; acknowledge it gently and note it for pastoral follow-up.

Section B — Living Among Others

Lesson 04: The Power of Godly Relationships

Facilitator’s aim: Move the group from treating community as optional to seeing godly relationships as essential, protective, and named directly by Scripture.

Key Scripture: 1 Corinthians 15:33 — Do not be deceived: "Evil company corrupts good habits."

Core Truth

Who you surround yourself with shapes who you become; godly relationships are not optional extras to the Christian life, they are how God grows and protects it.

Watch for:

  • The opening question can expose real isolation. Do not let anyone answer performatively; press gently for honesty.
  • Distinguish clearly between confessing struggles for accountability (James 5:16) and confessing sin for forgiveness (which belongs to God alone); this is easy to blur.
  • Someone may use “evil company” to justify withdrawing from all non-believing relationships. Clarify: this is about close, formative company, not loving people far from God.

Pastoral note

Watch for members who are lonely but not isolated by choice, illness, distance, bereavement. The lesson's challenge (“who do you walk with?”) can wound rather than convict if handled without care.

Lesson 05: Persecution

Facilitator’s aim: Remove the shock of opposition by establishing persecution as expected, not exceptional, while keeping the group from either naivety or bitterness.

Key Scripture: 2 Timothy 3:12 — Yes, and all who desire to live godly in Christ Jesus will suffer persecution.

Core Truth

Persecution is not a sign that something has gone wrong; it is the expected result of living faithfully for Christ in a world that opposed Him first.

Watch for:

  • Some members may never have faced real opposition and can feel the lesson does not apply to them. Broaden gently to quieter forms: mockery, exclusion, awkward silence.
  • The Ephesians 6:12 point about spiritual opposition can be misused to dismiss legitimate personal responsibility in a conflict. Keep both truths in view.
  • Galatians 6:12's point about avoiding persecution through compromise is convicting; give it room rather than rushing past it.

Pastoral note

If a member is facing real, current persecution (family rejection, workplace hostility), this lesson can reopen real pain. Follow up privately rather than processing it fully in the group setting.

Lesson 06: The King and His Kingdom

Facilitator’s aim: Reframe the Kingdom of God from a future destination or a political idea into a present, personal rule that is also still to come in fullness.

Key Scripture: Luke 17:21 — Nor will they say, "See here!" or "See there!" For indeed, the kingdom of God is within you.

Core Truth

The Kingdom of God is not primarily a place or a political system; it is God’s rule breaking into human lives now, with a future, visible consummation still to come.

Watch for:

  • The Israel/kingship history can run long. Keep it brisk; it exists to set up the anointing and the Messiah, not to become an Old Testament survey.
  • Some may equate “Kingdom” with the institutional church. Distinguish the two clearly using Luke 17:21.
  • The present/future tension (“already, not yet”) is genuinely difficult. Do not force premature resolution; let the group sit with both halves.

Pastoral note

This lesson can surface disappointment for those expecting visible, dramatic Kingdom breakthroughs (in health, provision, circumstance) that have not yet come. Hold both hope and patience together.

Section C — Living Free from Law and Sin

Lesson 07: The Object of Saving Faith

Facilitator’s aim: Distinguish saving faith in Christ Himself from a transactional faith in His benefits, using the Tozer quotation as the session's anchor.

Key Scripture: Acts 16:31 — So they said, "Believe on the Lord Jesus Christ, and you will be saved, you and your household."

Core Truth

Saving faith is faith in Christ Himself, not merely in what He can do for you; accepting His benefits while resisting His lordship is not the faith the New Testament describes.

Watch for:

  • This lesson can feel abstract. Bring it down to earth quickly: ask what specific benefit each person is most tempted to want without the lordship attached.
  • Some may hear this as a threat to assurance of salvation. Clarify: this is about the nature of genuine faith, not about doubting a settled decision made in sincerity.
  • The wedding analogy works well; let it do the heavy lifting rather than over-explaining it.

Pastoral note

Watch for members whose faith history includes a transactional “fire insurance” conversion. This lesson can prompt a healthy deepening, not alarm, if framed as growth rather than correction.

Lesson 08: The Proper Use of God's Law

Facilitator’s aim: Establish the Law as diagnostic, not curative, so the group stops striving to earn what only Christ's righteousness can give.

Key Scripture: Romans 3:20 — Therefore by the deeds of the law no flesh will be justified in His sight, for by the law is the knowledge of sin.

Core Truth

God’s Law was never given as a path to righteousness; it was given to reveal sin and expose the need for a Saviour.

Watch for:

  • James 2:10 (“guilty of all”) can feel harsh out of context. Move quickly to Romans 10:4 so the session does not end on condemnation.
  • Jesus's heart-level reading of the commandments (Matthew 5) can trigger real anxiety in perfectionist members. Reassure: the point is need, not despair.
  • Some will want to debate the role of the Law in the Christian life generally. Keep the session anchored to this lesson's specific claim: diagnostic, not a path to righteousness.

Pastoral note

This lesson often surfaces old, performance-based faith patterns. Watch for someone realising mid-session they have been quietly trying to earn God's approval for years.

Lesson 09: Not Under Law, but Under Grace

Facilitator’s aim: Redefine grace as favour plus ability, not only pardon, so the group leans on it for ongoing living, not just past forgiveness.

Key Scripture: Romans 6:14 — For sin shall not have dominion over you, for you are not under law but under grace.

Core Truth

Grace is not merely forgiveness for past failure; it is God’s unearned, undeserved favour and ability, freely given, that replaces performance as the basis of standing with Him.

Watch for:

  • The “grace is too easy” objection surfaces often; have Romans 6:14's own answer ready: grace breaks sin's dominion, it does not excuse it.
  • Some conflate grace with low expectations of holiness. Distinguish: grace supplies the ability for holiness; it does not lower the standard.
  • The “defying gravity” image is memorable; use it, but pair it with Romans 5:17 so the lesson lands on gift, not just impossibility.

Pastoral note

Watch for members still functionally living under law, measuring God's favour by recent behaviour. This lesson can be the turning point; give it room rather than rushing to the next point.

Lesson 10: No More Consciousness of Sin

Facilitator’s aim: Dismantle lingering, unresolved guilt over forgiven sin by establishing the sufficiency and finality of Christ's one sacrifice.

Key Scripture: Hebrews 10:14 — For by one offering He has perfected forever those who are being sanctified.

Core Truth

Christ’s single sacrifice was sufficient to deal with sin permanently; believers are no longer meant to carry an ongoing consciousness of guilt.

Watch for:

  • This is one of the more pastorally sensitive lessons in Part 3. Some members will recognise themselves immediately; give real space rather than moving briskly through the points.
  • Distinguish clearly between conviction that leads to repentance (healthy) and lingering sin-consciousness that denies the cross (the lesson's target).
  • Hebrews 10's repetition argument can feel technical. Ground it quickly in the lesson's own opening illustration about perpetual reminders creating bondage.

Pastoral note

Expect this lesson to surface old, specific guilt some members have carried for years. Do not rush to move on; let Hebrews 8:12 be spoken over the room, not just read.

Lesson 11: I Am Loved, I Am Pretty

Facilitator’s aim: Anchor identity and self-worth in Christ's finished work rather than in behaviour, using Romans 8:38-39's exhaustive list as the session's centre.

Key Scripture: Romans 5:19 — For as by one man’s disobedience many were made sinners, so also by one Man’s obedience many will be made righteous.

Core Truth

God’s love for the believer is already complete and cannot be increased by good behaviour or decreased by failure; righteousness in Christ is a permanent gift, not a fluctuating balance.

Watch for:

  • This lesson is personal, not merely doctrinal. Resist over-intellectualising it; let it reach actual self-worth, not just theology.
  • Some will hear “God can't love you any more” as minimising the seriousness of sin. Clarify: this is about the ceiling on love, not a licence for indifference to sin.
  • The lesson's title is deliberately personal and vulnerable. Honour that tone; this is not the session for clinical detachment.

Pastoral note

Watch closely for members carrying real, long-standing self-worth wounds. This lesson can be the most significant one in the whole series for some people; do not rush the close.

Section D — Living as a Disciple

Lesson 12: The Fruit of Salvation (Part 1)

Facilitator’s aim: Establish fruit as evidence of genuine faith, not payment for it, and press the group to an honest look at what their own lives are producing.

Key Scripture: James 2:18 — But someone will say, "You have faith, and I have works." Show me your faith without your works, and I will show you my faith by my works.

Core Truth

Genuine saving faith is not a one-time decision that ends; it is a continuous, living trust that naturally produces visible fruit over time.

Watch for:

  • The Calvinism/Arminianism reference in the source material is background, not a debate to import into the room. Keep the focus on fruit as evidence, not on resolving that theological question.
  • James 2:17 (“faith without works is dead”) can be heard as contradicting salvation by grace. Clarify quickly: James describes what living faith looks like, not a second requirement for salvation.
  • This lesson opens Section D; set the tone that the remaining five lessons build directly on each other.

Pastoral note

Some will hear this lesson as an invitation to self-audit their salvation anxiously. Redirect toward grounded assurance: fruit is evidence to notice with gratitude, not a pass/fail test to fear.

Lesson 13: The Fruit of Salvation (Part 2)

Facilitator’s aim: Give the group concrete, observable tests for assurance (love, righteousness, belief) while keeping diligence from tipping into anxiety.

Key Scripture: 1 John 3:14 — We know that we have passed from death to life, because we love the brethren. He who does not love his brother abides in death.

Core Truth

Believers can recognise the reality of their own salvation, and see it in others, through observable evidence: righteous living, love, and holiness, not merely through claimed belief.

Watch for:

  • Simon the sorcerer's story (Acts 8) is sobering; let it land, but close by returning to 1 John 5:13's purpose: that you may know, not that you may doubt.
  • “Diligence” in 2 Peter 1:10 can be misheard as earning assurance through effort. Clarify: diligence confirms what is already true, it does not create it.
  • Watch the pace; this lesson pairs closely with Lesson 12 and can feel repetitive if not distinguished clearly (Lesson 12: fruit generally; Lesson 13: love and righteousness specifically, plus assurance).

Pastoral note

If anyone in the group has struggled with assurance of salvation, this lesson is an opportunity for real ministry. Consider following up individually rather than only in the group setting.

Lesson 14: A Call to Discipleship

Facilitator’s aim: Distinguish making converts from making disciples, and move at least one member toward a concrete next step in discipling someone else.

Key Scripture: Luke 14:33 — So likewise, whoever of you does not forsake all that he has cannot be My disciple.

Core Truth

Christ’s commission was never merely to make converts; it was to make disciples, transformed believers capable of reproducing genuine faith in others.

Watch for:

  • Luke 14:33's “forsake all” language is strong; do not soften it, but be ready to unpack what it means practically rather than leaving it abstract.
  • The multiplication illustration is powerful but can feel like a numbers game if not handled carefully. Keep the emphasis on relationship, not statistics.
  • Some will feel unqualified to disciple anyone. Reassure using the lesson's own point: discipleship is personal investment, not formal expertise.

Pastoral note

This lesson is a natural point to identify who in the group might be ready to begin discipling someone else. Consider a direct, private follow-up conversation with one or two members.

Lesson 15: How to Use Your Testimony

Facilitator’s aim: Get every member to draft and practise a two-minute version of their own testimony by the end of the session.

Key Scripture: Acts 26:16 — But rise and stand on your feet; for I have appeared to you for this purpose, to make you a minister and a witness both of the things which you have seen and of the things which I will yet reveal to you.

Core Truth

A personal testimony, told simply and honestly, is one of the most effective and accessible evangelism tools every believer already has.

Watch for:

  • Some will feel their testimony is “not dramatic enough.” Affirm directly: ongoing transformation is as valid a testimony as a crisis conversion.
  • Watch the time; if members share testimonies aloud, it can run long. Consider pairs rather than the whole group if time is tight.
  • Distinguish relational, patient evangelism from pressuring a decision. Some members may associate testimony-sharing with the latter; reframe using Acts 16:14's patient pattern.

Pastoral note

This is a practical, doing lesson, not just a discussing one. Do not let it end without every member having actually attempted a draft, even a rough one, before they leave.

Lesson 16: Using Everyone's Gifts to Disciple

Facilitator’s aim: Close the whole 48-lesson series by mobilising every member toward one concrete act of ministry using their own gift, not leaving outreach to a gifted few.

Key Scripture: Ephesians 4:12 — For the equipping of the saints for the work of ministry, for the edifying of the body of Christ.

Core Truth

Discipleship and evangelism were never meant to rest on a gifted few; every believer’s specific gift has a role to play in building up the church and reaching others.

Watch for:

  • This is the synthesis lesson for Part 3 and the whole series. Take time to look back across all three Parts, not only Part 3's four sections.
  • The statistic about few Christians evangelising can land as guilt rather than diagnosis. Frame it as this lesson's opening does: naming the gap so it can close, not shaming anyone in the room.
  • Resist the urge to introduce new content here. This lesson's job is synthesis and commissioning, not fresh teaching.

Pastoral note

This is the natural point to close the whole series meaningfully; consider a dedicated time of commissioning or prayer over each member's identified gift and next step, not just a normal session close.
