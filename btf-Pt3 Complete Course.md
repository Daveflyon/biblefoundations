# Lesson 01: The Divine Flow
*Lesson 1 of 16  |  Section A: Living in the Spirit's Power*

### Opening Question
**Think of a time you felt a sudden, unexplained pull to help, comfort or speak to someone you barely knew. What did you do with that prompting, and what stopped you or moved you?**

### Key Scripture
**Philemon 1:6**
> *That the sharing of your faith may become effective by the acknowledgment of every good thing which is in you in Christ Jesus.*

*Read this slowly before moving on. The flow this lesson describes starts here: with acknowledging what is already true, not with manufacturing something new.*

### Core Truth
**God’s love is not something you work up. It is something already placed in you by the Holy Spirit, waiting to be acknowledged and released.**

### Bible Teaching

#### Something already there, not something to build

Every believer already has "every good thing" in Christ Jesus placed within them. The starting point is not manufacturing love or compassion through willpower; it is recognising what the Holy Spirit has already deposited. Andrew Wommack describes this first step as being like "ringing a bell" rather than building something from nothing.

**1 John 4:7-8**
> *Beloved, let us love one another, for love is of God; and everyone who loves is born of God and knows God. He who does not love does not know God, for God is love.*

God’s love, once poured into the heart, is meant to move outward, not stay contained. This is not a suggestion for especially spiritual believers; it is the ordinary nature of anyone born of God.

#### Testing the source: 1 Corinthians 13

Not every strong feeling toward another person is the divine flow. Supernatural compassion can be told apart from ordinary sentiment, sympathy, or self-interested attraction by testing it against the description of love in 1 Corinthians 13.

**1 Corinthians 13:4-5**
> *Love suffers long and is kind; love does not envy; love does not parade itself, is not puffed up; does not behave rudely, does not seek its own, is not provoked, thinks no evil.*

**Going Deeper:** Wommack’s teaching draws a careful line between emotional sympathy and the divine flow. Sympathy can be self-focused; it often centres on how the situation makes you feel. The divine flow is always aimed outward, toward the other person’s genuine good, and it is tested by whether it actually looks like 1 Corinthians 13 in practice, not merely by its intensity.

#### Christ’s pattern: compassion, then action

Jesus modelled the divine flow constantly. Before He acted, He felt something first.

**Matthew 14:14**
> *And Jesus went out and saw a great multitude; and He was moved with compassion for them, and healed their sick.*

Feeling compassion is the start of the flow, not its completion. The flow is not finished until it is released in a word, a touch, or an action, the way Jesus released His compassion in healing, and the way He released forgiveness itself from the cross.

**Luke 23:34**
> *Then Jesus said, "Father, forgive them, for they do not know what they do."*

**Note:** The flow is designed to reach the other person, not stop at recognition. Withholding the word or deed once prompted leaves the flow incomplete, whatever was genuinely felt in the moment.


### Key Discovery Questions

1. Describe a specific moment when you sensed a prompting to show love or compassion to someone. What did you do?

2. How would you use 1 Corinthians 13:4-8 to test whether a feeling is the divine flow or something else?

3. Philemon 1:6 says "every good thing" is already in you in Christ. What does that change about how you approach showing love to others?

4. What is one prompting you have felt recently but not yet acted on? What would completing that flow look like this week?

### Group Discussion Questions

1. Why do you think Wommack compares acknowledging what is already in you to "ringing a bell" rather than "building" something new?

2. Share a time someone’s compassion toward you clearly went beyond ordinary kindness. What made it feel supernatural?

3. What stops believers most often from completing the flow once they sense it: fear, self-doubt, timing, or something else?

4. How does knowing love is already in you change the pressure you might feel to "perform" ministry to others?

### Application

The divine flow is not a truth to admire from a distance; it is meant to move through you into someone else’s life this week.

- **Personal life.** Before your next opportunity to help someone, pause and name what you are sensing: is this compassion that matches 1 Corinthians 13, or something else? Practise the discernment, not just the doing.

- **Church community.** Choose one prompting you have been sitting on and follow it through in a word, a touch, or a practical act, however small, for someone in your church community this week.

- **Difficult seasons.** When compassion feels hard to access in a difficult season of your own, read through one Gospel account of Jesus healing or helping someone and let His pattern, compassion first, then action, recalibrate your own.

- **Scripture to carry.** Memorise Philemon 1:6: "That the sharing of your faith may become effective by the acknowledgment of every good thing which is in you in Christ Jesus." Carry it as the reminder that the flow starts with acknowledgment, not effort.

### Revision Cards

  **A:** "That the sharing of your faith may become effective by the acknowledgment of every good thing which is in you in Christ Jesus." Philemon 1:6

- **Q:** What is God’s own nature, according to 1 John? (1 John 4:8)  
  **A:** "He who does not love does not know God, for God is love." 1 John 4:8

- **Q:** What test distinguishes divine compassion from self-interest? (1 Corinthians 13:4)  
  **A:** "Love suffers long and is kind; love does not envy; love does not parade itself, is not puffed up." 1 Corinthians 13:4

- **Q:** What did Jesus feel before He healed the multitudes? (Matthew 14:14)  
  **A:** "And Jesus went out and saw a great multitude; and He was moved with compassion for them, and healed their sick." Matthew 14:14

- **Q:** What did Jesus pray for those crucifying Him? (Luke 23:34)  
  **A:** "Then Jesus said, ‘Father, forgive them, for they do not know what they do.’" Luke 23:34

# Lesson 02: Using the Gifts to Minister
*Lesson 2 of 16  |  Section A: Living in the Spirit's Power*

### Opening Question
**If someone asked you "what is your spiritual gift?" right now, could you answer? What has stopped you from identifying or using it so far?**

### Key Scripture
**1 Peter 4:11**
> *If anyone speaks, let him speak as the oracles of God. If anyone ministers, let him do it as with the ability which God supplies, that in all things God may be glorified through Jesus Christ.*

*Read this slowly before moving on. Notice where the ability is said to come from: supplied, not self-generated.*

### Core Truth
**Every believer has been given a specific gift by the Holy Spirit, and ministry is meant to flow through God’s supplied ability, not your own natural talent.**

### Bible Teaching

#### A gift for every believer, not a gifted few

The Spirit gives gifts "to each one individually as He wills." Every believer has at least one gift, not only pastors or recognised leaders.

**1 Corinthians 12:7, 11**
> *But the manifestation of the Spirit is given to each one for the profit of all... But one and the same Spirit works all these things, distributing to each one individually as He wills.*

Gifts differ in kind but serve one purpose: the profit of all, not personal status or recognition.

#### The breadth of the gifts

1 Corinthians 12 lists nine gifts: word of wisdom, word of knowledge, faith, gifts of healing, working of miracles, prophecy, discerning of spirits, tongues, and interpretation of tongues. Romans 12 adds further gifts of service, teaching, exhortation, giving, leading and mercy.

**Going Deeper:** The distinction between the nine gifts in 1 Corinthians 12 and the six in Romans 12 matters practically. The Corinthians list leans toward the more overtly supernatural: healing, miracles, tongues. The Romans list includes gifts many believers already exercise without naming them as spiritual gifts at all, such as giving, leading and showing mercy.

#### Ministering from supplied ability, not natural skill

Ministering "as with the ability which God supplies" means leaning on God’s strength in the moment, not rehearsed natural skill. This does not exclude natural talent; it means natural talent is not the engine.

**Hebrews 2:3-4**
> *God also bearing witness both with signs and wonders, with various miracles, and gifts of the Holy Spirit, according to His own will.*

#### Gifts grow through use

Recognising a gift and never stepping out in it leaves it dormant. Paul’s own ministry continued even in difficult circumstances, such as the shipwreck voyage in Acts 27, where he continued to minister faith and encouragement to those around him. Even imperfect first attempts, offered sincerely, are how believers grow into their gifting.

**Note:** You do not need to feel fully confident before you begin. Gifts are sharpened in the exercising of them, not perfected in private before they are ever used.


### Key Discovery Questions

1. Which of the gifts listed in 1 Corinthians 12:8-10 or Romans 12:6-8 do you recognise in your own life, even if you have never called it a "spiritual gift"?

2. What is the difference between ministering from natural talent and ministering "as with the ability which God supplies" (1 Peter 4:11)?

3. Why does 1 Corinthians 12:7 say gifts are given "for the profit of all" rather than for the individual?

4. What is one small step you could take this week to exercise a gift you have been holding back from using?

### Group Discussion Questions

1. Why do you think so many believers assume spiritual gifts belong only to pastors or especially gifted people?

2. Share a time you saw someone minister to you in a way that clearly went beyond their natural ability. What made it stand out?

3. How can a church make room for quieter gifts (mercy, giving, service) to be recognised as much as more visible ones (teaching, healing)?

4. What holds people back most from ever finding out what their gift is: fear of failure, lack of opportunity, or something else?

### Application

A gift left unused stays theoretical. This week, move from naming your gift to exercising it, even in a small way.

- **Personal life.** Read through 1 Corinthians 12:8-10 and Romans 12:6-8 this week and write down which gift, or gifts, you most recognise in yourself.

- **Church community.** Look for one low-stakes opportunity this week to exercise that gift within your church community, even imperfectly.

- **Difficult seasons.** When you feel unqualified or hesitant to step out, remember that gifts are sharpened through use, not before it. Take the first step anyway, trusting God’s supplied ability rather than your own confidence.

- **Scripture to carry.** Memorise 1 Peter 4:11: "If anyone speaks, let him speak as the oracles of God. If anyone ministers, let him do it as with the ability which God supplies." Let it reset your posture from self-reliance to Spirit-reliance.

### Revision Cards

- **Q:** How should a believer minister, according to 1 Peter 4:11?  
  **A:** "As with the ability which God supplies, that in all things God may be glorified through Jesus Christ." 1 Peter 4:11

- **Q:** Who decides which gift each believer receives? (1 Corinthians 12:11)  
  **A:** "One and the same Spirit, distributing to each one individually as He wills." 1 Corinthians 12:11

- **Q:** What is the stated purpose of spiritual gifts? (1 Corinthians 12:7)  
  **A:** "But the manifestation of the Spirit is given to each one for the profit of all." 1 Corinthians 12:7

- **Q:** Name three gifts listed in Romans 12:6-8.  
  **A:** Prophecy, ministry (service), teaching, exhortation, giving, leading, and mercy are listed in Romans 12:6-8.

- **Q:** Name three of the nine gifts in 1 Corinthians 12:8-10.  
  **A:** Word of wisdom, word of knowledge, faith, gifts of healing, working of miracles, prophecy, discerning of spirits, tongues, and interpretation of tongues.

- **Q:** What should believers "seek to excel in", according to Paul? (1 Corinthians 14:12)  
  **A:** "Since you are zealous for spiritual gifts, let it be for the edification of the church that you seek to excel." 1 Corinthians 14:12

# Lesson 03: Miracles Glorify God
*Lesson 3 of 16  |  Section A: Living in the Spirit's Power*

### Opening Question
**When you hear about a modern-day miracle, healing or answered prayer, what is your first instinct: excitement, scepticism, or something in between? Why?**

### Key Scripture
**Matthew 9:8**
> *Now when the multitudes saw it, they marvelled and glorified God, who had given such power to men.*

*Read this slowly before moving on. Notice who received the crowd’s response: not the man who acted, but God.*

### Core Truth
**Miracles are never given for the minister’s credit; they exist to point people to God and confirm His Word as true.**

### Bible Teaching

#### Where the glory goes

When Jesus healed the paralytic, the crowd’s response was not to praise Jesus the man but to glorify God. The miracle’s purpose was always upward, toward God, not toward the one He worked through.

**Mark 2:5**
> *When Jesus saw their faith, He said to the paralytic, "Son, your sins are forgiven you."*

Jesus first addressed the deeper issue, forgiveness of sin, and the physical healing followed as proof of His authority to forgive. The visible miracle proved the invisible authority.

#### Miracles confirm the Word

Miracles serve as confirmation of the spoken Word, not as unrelated spectacle separated from the message being preached.

**Hebrews 2:4**
> *God also bearing witness both with signs and wonders, with various miracles, and gifts of the Holy Spirit, according to His own will.*

**Mark 16:20**
> *And they went out and preached everywhere, the Lord working with them and confirming the word through the accompanying signs. Amen.*

#### Demonstration, not persuasion

The apostles’ ministry pattern was never rhetorical polish; it was demonstration of the Spirit and of power, so that faith would rest on God, not on human eloquence.

**1 Corinthians 2:4**
> *And my speech and my preaching were not with persuasive words of human wisdom, but in demonstration of the Spirit and of power.*

**Going Deeper:** Wommack’s phrase "like ringing the dinner bell" captures a central caution: a miracle is meant to draw people to the meal, the Gospel itself, not to become the meal. A ministry that chases signs for their own sake has mistaken the invitation for the feast.

#### The same power, still available

Because Jesus Christ is the same yesterday, today, and forever, the same power at work in the Gospels is available to the church now, not confined to the apostolic era.

**Hebrews 13:8**
> *Jesus Christ is the same yesterday, today, and forever.*

**Note:** Expecting God to work in power today is not presumption; it is consistent with who Scripture says Christ still is.


### Key Discovery Questions

1. Why did the crowd in Matthew 9:8 glorify God rather than praise Jesus personally, even though they had just watched Him act?

2. How does Mark 2:1-12 show that physical healing was proof of a deeper spiritual authority?

3. What does it mean, practically, for ministry to be a "demonstration of the Spirit and of power" rather than persuasive argument (1 Corinthians 2:4)?

4. If Jesus Christ is "the same yesterday, today, and forever," what does that suggest about what believers today should expect God to do?

### Group Discussion Questions

1. Why do you think Wommack describes miracles as being "like ringing the dinner bell" rather than the meal itself?

2. Have you witnessed or experienced something you believed was a genuine miracle? What was your response afterward?

3. How can a church guard against making miracles about the reputation of the person used, rather than about God?

4. What is the danger, according to this lesson, in assuming miracles were only for Bible times?

### Application

This week, practise redirecting credit to God and expecting Him to work, in ordinary moments as much as dramatic ones.

- **Personal life.** Notice any moment where you or someone else did something meaningful for another person, and practise consciously redirecting the credit to God rather than absorbing it.

- **Church community.** Ask God for an opportunity to pray for someone’s healing or need this week, trusting Him for the outcome rather than your own ability.

- **Difficult seasons.** When circumstances feel beyond help, read one Gospel healing account slowly and notice what the healed person’s response was, letting it renew your own expectation of God.

- **Scripture to carry.** Memorise Hebrews 13:8: "Jesus Christ is the same yesterday, today, and forever." Let it be the foundation for expecting God to still work in power now.

### Revision Cards

- **Q:** What was the crowd’s response to the paralytic’s healing? (Matthew 9:8)  
  **A:** "Now when the multitudes saw it, they marvelled and glorified God, who had given such power to men." Matthew 9:8

- **Q:** How does God bear witness to the Word, according to Hebrews? (Hebrews 2:4)  
  **A:** "God also bearing witness both with signs and wonders, with various miracles, and gifts of the Holy Spirit." Hebrews 2:4

- **Q:** What did Jesus address before healing the paralytic’s body? (Mark 2:5)  
  **A:** "When Jesus saw their faith, He said to the paralytic, ‘Son, your sins are forgiven you.’" Mark 2:5

- **Q:** How did Paul describe his own preaching method? (1 Corinthians 2:4)  
  **A:** "My speech and my preaching were not with persuasive words of human wisdom, but in demonstration of the Spirit and of power." 1 Corinthians 2:4

- **Q:** What is true of Jesus Christ across all time? (Hebrews 13:8)  
  **A:** "Jesus Christ is the same yesterday, today, and forever." Hebrews 13:8

- **Q:** With what was Jesus anointed for ministry? (Acts 10:38)  
  **A:** "God anointed Jesus of Nazareth with the Holy Spirit and with power, who went about doing good and healing all who were oppressed by the devil, for God was with Him." Acts 10:38

# Lesson 04: The Power of Godly Relationships
*Lesson 4 of 16  |  Section B: Living Among Others*

### Opening Question
**Think about the people you spend the most time with. Are they, on balance, pulling you closer to God or quietly pulling you away? How would you actually know?**

### Key Scripture
**1 Corinthians 15:33**
> *Do not be deceived: "Evil company corrupts good habits."*

*Read this slowly before moving on. Deception is the word Paul uses; this is not a truth we naturally believe without being told.*

### Core Truth
**Who you surround yourself with shapes who you become; godly relationships are not optional extras to the Christian life, they are how God grows and protects it.**

### Bible Teaching

#### Called out together, not called out alone

The New Testament word for church, ekklesia, means "a called out group." Christianity was never designed to be lived in isolation from other believers.

**1 Corinthians 12:12**
> *For as the body is one and has many members, but all the members of that one body, being many, are one body, so also is Christ.*

The body of Christ functions like a physical body: each member has a distinct gift and role, and the body suffers when members disconnect from one another.

#### Confession and accountability

Confession of struggles to trustworthy, godly people produces healing and accountability. This is different from confession of sin for forgiveness, which belongs to God alone.

**James 5:16**
> *Confess your trespasses to one another, and pray for one another, that you may be healed. The effective, fervent prayer of a righteous man avails much.*

**Going Deeper:** Confessing sin to God alone secures forgiveness (1 John 1:9), but confessing struggles to trusted, godly people (James 5:16) secures something different: ongoing accountability and healing that a private relationship with God, on its own, was never designed to replace.

#### Exhortation and the danger of drifting

Scripture commands believers to exhort one another daily and warns against forsaking the assembling of believers, because neglecting it leaves the heart open to being hardened through the deceitfulness of sin.

**Hebrews 10:25**
> *Not forsaking the assembling of ourselves together, as is the manner of some, but exhorting one another, and so much the more as you see the Day approaching.*

#### Company shapes character

Ungodly company is not neutral; it actively corrupts good habits and thinking. This is why Scripture repeatedly warns against close association with those who will pull a believer away from God.

**Proverbs 13:20**
> *He who walks with wise men will be wise, but the companion of fools will be destroyed.*

**Caution:** This is not a call to abandon relationships with those who do not yet know Christ; loving people far from God is part of the Christian life. It is a warning about close, formative company that consistently pulls you toward compromise rather than growth.


### Key Discovery Questions

1. Who are the two or three people who most exhort or challenge you spiritually? When did you last let them into a real struggle?

2. What is the difference between confessing sin to God for forgiveness and confessing struggles to another believer for accountability (James 5:16)?

3. Proverbs 13:20 says "he who walks with wise men will be wise." Look honestly at who you walk with most; what is it shaping in you?

4. What is one step you could take this week to be less isolated in your walk with Christ?

### Group Discussion Questions

1. Why do you think the earliest church is described as devoting itself to "fellowship" as its own distinct practice, alongside teaching and prayer (Acts 2:42)?

2. Share a time a godly relationship changed the direction of a struggle you were facing.

3. What makes it hard to confess a real struggle to another believer, even one we trust?

4. How do you discern the line between loving people who are far from God and letting "evil company" corrupt your own habits (1 Corinthians 15:33)?

### Application

Godly relationships are not automatic; they take deliberate honesty and presence. Take one real step this week.

- **Personal life.** List the three to five people you spend the most time with. Honestly assess whether that company is drawing you toward or away from God.

- **Church community.** Share one real struggle with a trustworthy, godly person this week rather than carrying it alone.

- **Difficult seasons.** If you have been drifting from corporate gathering, commit to being present this week specifically, not out of obligation but as protection for your own heart (Hebrews 10:25).

- **Scripture to carry.** Memorise 1 Corinthians 15:33: "Do not be deceived: ‘Evil company corrupts good habits.’" Let it prompt an honest look at your closest relationships.

# Lesson 05: Persecution
*Lesson 5 of 16  |  Section B: Living Among Others*

### Opening Question
**Have you ever softened what you believe, or stayed quiet about your faith, to avoid conflict or discomfort? What made you hold back?**

### Key Scripture
**2 Timothy 3:12**
> *Yes, and all who desire to live godly in Christ Jesus will suffer persecution.*

*Read this slowly before moving on. Notice the word "will," not "might."*

### Core Truth
**Persecution is not a sign that something has gone wrong; it is the expected result of living faithfully for Christ in a world that opposed Him first.**

### Bible Teaching

#### Expected, not accidental

Jesus told His disciples plainly that He was sending them out into opposition. Persecution was anticipated, not accidental.

**Matthew 10:16**
> *Behold, I send you out as sheep in the midst of wolves. Therefore be wise as serpents and harmless as doves.*

Facing opposition calls for a specific posture: wisdom and gentleness held together, neither naive nor retaliatory.

#### A promise, not merely a possibility

Persecution is promised, not merely possible, for all who desire to live godly in Christ Jesus. Godly living itself, not failure, is what draws this particular resistance.

**2 Corinthians 11:23-24**
> *Are they ministers of Christ? I speak as a fool, I am more: in labors more abundant, in stripes above measure, in prisons more frequently, in deaths often. From the Jews five times I received forty stripes minus one.*

#### The real opposition is spiritual

The real opposition behind human persecution is spiritual. Believers do not wrestle against flesh and blood but against spiritual forces working through people.

**Ephesians 6:12**
> *For we do not wrestle against flesh and blood, but against principalities, against powers, against the rulers of the darkness of this age, against spiritual hosts of wickedness in the heavenly places.*

**Note:** Recognising the deeper spiritual conflict does not excuse mistreatment or turn opponents into enemies to be despised; it reframes the response, praying and standing firm rather than only reacting personally.

#### Persecution spreads the Gospel

Persecution has historically scattered and spread the Gospel rather than silencing it.

**Acts 8:1, 4**
> *At that time a great persecution arose against the church which was at Jerusalem... Therefore those who were scattered went everywhere preaching the word.*

**Going Deeper:** Galatians 6:12 adds an uncomfortable but important angle: some avoid persecution specifically by compromising the offence of the Gospel, watering down doctrine that would otherwise provoke opposition. Some absence of persecution is not peace; it is compromise.


### Key Discovery Questions

1. When have you experienced any form of resistance, mockery or exclusion because of your faith? How did you respond?

2. What does it mean, practically, to be "wise as serpents and harmless as doves" (Matthew 10:16) when facing opposition?

3. How does knowing the real conflict is spiritual, not merely personal, change how you view people who oppose your faith (Ephesians 6:12)?

4. Galatians 6:12 suggests some avoid persecution by softening the Gospel. Where might you be tempted to do the same?

### Group Discussion Questions

1. Why do you think Jesus prepared His disciples for persecution so directly, rather than promising them ease?

2. Share a time you saw someone’s faith grow stronger, not weaker, because of opposition.

3. What is the danger in either extreme: being naive about opposition, or becoming bitter and retaliatory toward it?

4. How should a believer discern the difference between suffering for righteousness and suffering as a consequence of their own poor choices?

### Application

Opposition, when it comes, is not evidence of failure. This week, practise steady faithfulness rather than retreat.

- **Personal life.** Identify one place you have quietly avoided speaking or living out your faith to escape discomfort, and pray for boldness there.

- **Church community.** Set aside time this week to pray specifically for believers facing real persecution elsewhere in the world.

- **Difficult seasons.** If you face pushback for your faith this week, practise responding with the "wise as serpents, harmless as doves" posture rather than silence or sharpness.

- **Scripture to carry.** Memorise 2 Timothy 3:12: "Yes, and all who desire to live godly in Christ Jesus will suffer persecution." Let it remove the surprise when opposition comes.

### Revision Cards

- **Q:** How did Jesus describe sending His disciples out? (Matthew 10:16)  
  **A:** "Behold, I send you out as sheep in the midst of wolves. Therefore be wise as serpents and harmless as doves." Matthew 10:16

- **Q:** Who will suffer persecution, according to Paul? (2 Timothy 3:12)  
  **A:** "Yes, and all who desire to live godly in Christ Jesus will suffer persecution." 2 Timothy 3:12

- **Q:** What do believers actually wrestle against? (Ephesians 6:12)  
  **A:** "We do not wrestle against flesh and blood, but against principalities, against powers, against the rulers of the darkness of this age." Ephesians 6:12

- **Q:** What happened after believers were scattered by persecution? (Acts 8:4)  
  **A:** "Therefore those who were scattered went everywhere preaching the word." Acts 8:4

- **Q:** What did Jesus say to Saul, who was persecuting the church? (Acts 9:4)  
  **A:** "Then he fell to the ground, and heard a voice saying to him, ‘Saul, Saul, why are you persecuting Me?’" Acts 9:4

# Lesson 06: The King and His Kingdom
*Lesson 6 of 16  |  Section B: Living Among Others*

### Opening Question
**When you hear the phrase "Kingdom of God," what image comes to mind? A place, a future event, a present reality, or something else?**

### Key Scripture
**Luke 17:21**
> *Nor will they say, "See here!" or "See there!" For indeed, the kingdom of God is within you.*

*Read this slowly before moving on. This is not the picture of the Kingdom many expect.*

### Core Truth
**The Kingdom of God is not primarily a place or a political system; it is God’s rule breaking into human lives now, with a future, visible consummation still to come.**

### Bible Teaching

#### From direct rule to an earthly king

Israel began as a theocracy under God’s direct rule. When the nation demanded an earthly king, God permitted Saul, then David, as visible representatives of His invisible rule.

**1 Samuel 8:7**
> *And the Lord said to Samuel, "Heed the voice of the people in all that they say to you; for they have not rejected you, but they have rejected Me, that I should not reign over them."*

A consistent pattern runs through Israel’s kings: following the Lord brought prosperity, and abandoning Him brought captivity and ruin.

#### The anointing and the Messiah

Kings were anointed with oil as a symbol of the Holy Spirit’s empowerment for their role.

**1 Samuel 16:13**
> *Then Samuel took the horn of oil and anointed him in the midst of his brothers; and the Spirit of the Lord came upon David from that day forward.*

**Note:** This pattern of anointing is the origin of the title Messiah, meaning "Anointed One": Mashiach in Hebrew, Christos in Greek.

#### The Kingdom as Jesus’s central message

The Kingdom of God was the central and consistent theme of Jesus’s own preaching, and the one subject He specifically instructed His disciples to proclaim.

**Luke 9:2**
> *He sent them to preach the kingdom of God and to heal the sick.*

Entry into this Kingdom requires repentance, a genuine turning from sin and toward God through Christ, which brings both forgiveness and eternal life.

**Mark 1:15**
> *And saying, "The time is fulfilled, and the kingdom of God is at hand. Repent, and believe in the gospel."*

#### Present now, consummated later

The Kingdom did not arrive with the display many in Israel expected: armies, a throne, immediate political rule. And it will not fully arrive that way until Christ’s return.

**Going Deeper:** In between, the Kingdom grows quietly, within and among believers, grace manifested in Jesus’s ministry rather than spectacle, destined for a future, glorious, visible consummation.

# Lesson 07: The Object of Saving Faith
*Lesson 7 of 16  |  Section C: Living Free from Law and Sin*

### Opening Question
**If you’re honest, is there any part of who Christ is, His authority over your choices, for instance, that you’d rather keep at arm’s length while still accepting His benefits?**

### Key Scripture
**Acts 16:31**
> *So they said, "Believe on the Lord Jesus Christ, and you will be saved, you and your household."*

*Read this slowly before moving on. Notice the full title used: "the Lord Jesus Christ," not simply "Jesus."*

### Core Truth
**Saving faith is faith in Christ Himself, not merely in what He can do for you; accepting His benefits while resisting His lordship is not the faith the New Testament describes.**

### Bible Teaching

#### Christ Himself, not merely His benefits

A.W. Tozer’s summary captures the centre of this lesson: the only true object of saving faith is none other than Christ Himself, not the "saviourhood" of Christ nor the "lordship" of Christ, but Christ Himself.

Faith that wants Christ’s rescue but resists His authority is a selective faith, accepting benefits while rejecting the Person they come from.

#### Receiving the whole Person

**John 1:12**
> *But as many as received Him, to them He gave the right to become children of God, to those who believe in His name.*

To "receive Him" is to receive the right to become a child of God: reception of the whole Person, not a transaction for selected services.

#### Confessing His lordship

**Romans 10:9**
> *That if you confess with your mouth the Lord Jesus and believe in your heart that God has raised Him from the dead, you will be saved.*

Confession of lordship is part of the saving confession itself, not an optional add-on for the especially devoted.

#### The whole Gospel, not a partial one

**Romans 1:3-4**
> *Concerning His Son Jesus Christ our Lord, who was born of the seed of David according to the flesh, and declared to be the Son of God with power according to the Spirit of holiness, by the resurrection from the dead.*

Paul’s own gospel summary ties belief inseparably to who Jesus is: His identity as the Son of God, descended from David, raised from the dead.

**Going Deeper:** A wedding analogy is worth sitting with: a bride who wanted only what her husband could provide, and none of who he actually is, would not be said to love him. Salvation is relationship with a Person, not procurement of benefits from Him.


### Key Discovery Questions

1. Tozer says the object of saving faith is "Christ Himself," not just His "saviourhood." What is the practical difference between those two?

2. Where might you be tempted to want Christ’s benefits (comfort, blessing, rescue) without submitting to His lordship?

3. Romans 10:9 ties confession of Jesus as Lord to saving faith. What does confessing "Jesus is Lord" cost you, practically, in daily life?

4. How does John 1:12 ("received Him") change the picture from a one-time transaction to an ongoing relationship?

### Group Discussion Questions

1. Why do you think Tozer calls selective acceptance of Christ’s benefits, without His lordship, a "modern-day heresy"?

2. Share an area where it is easier to trust Christ for help than to trust Him for direction.

3. How does the wedding analogy (wanting the benefits of a spouse without loving the person) illuminate this lesson?

4. What would change in how we evangelise if we led with "Jesus is Lord" as clearly as we lead with "Jesus saves"?

### Application

Genuine faith is in Christ Himself. This week, examine where your own faith may be selective.

- **Personal life.** Ask honestly whether there is any part of Christ’s authority you have kept at arm’s length while still wanting His benefits.

- **Church community.** Practise Romans 10:9 deliberately this week: confess "Jesus is Lord" over one specific area of decision-making, not just belief in general.

- **Difficult seasons.** When you want rescue more than direction, read John 1:12 slowly and sit with what it means to "receive Him," the whole Person, not a set of services rendered.

- **Scripture to carry.** Memorise Acts 16:31: "Believe on the Lord Jesus Christ, and you will be saved." Let "Lord" stay attached to "Jesus Christ" every time you say it.

# Lesson 08: The Proper Use of God's Law
*Lesson 8 of 16  |  Section C: Living Free from Law and Sin*

### Opening Question
**Have you ever tried to earn a clear conscience by simply "being good enough"? How did that attempt end?**

### Key Scripture
**Romans 3:20**
> *Therefore by the deeds of the law no flesh will be justified in His sight, for by the law is the knowledge of sin.*

*Read this slowly before moving on. Notice what the Law is said to produce here: knowledge, not righteousness.*

### Core Truth
**God’s Law was never given as a path to righteousness; it was given to reveal sin and expose the need for a Saviour.**

### Bible Teaching

#### A conscience for everyone

Every person carries an innate moral conscience through which God speaks to human accountability, even apart from the written Law.

**Romans 2:14-15**
> *For when Gentiles, who do not have the law, by nature do the things in the law, these, although not having the law, are a law to themselves, who show the work of the law written in their hearts.*

#### The Law’s true standard

The Ten Commandments expose the true standard of God’s holiness. Jesus intensified this by tracing commandments like murder and adultery back to the heart itself.

**Matthew 5:21-22**
> *You have heard that it was said to those of old, "You shall not murder... But I say to you that whoever is angry with his brother without a cause shall be in danger of the judgment."*

#### Diagnostic, not curative

By the law is the knowledge of sin. The Law’s function is diagnostic, showing what is wrong; it was never designed to solve it.

**James 2:10**
> *For whoever shall keep the whole law, and yet stumble in one point, he is guilty of all.*

No one can achieve salvation through partial or even near-total obedience. The Law’s standard is all-or-nothing, which is precisely why it cannot save.

#### Christ, the end of the Law for righteousness

**Romans 10:4**
> *For Christ is the end of the law for righteousness to everyone who believes.*

The solution the Law itself cannot provide is found in Christ. His righteousness is given as a free gift, not earned by performance.

**Going Deeper:** The Law functioned as a tutor to bring us to Christ (Galatians 3:24). Understanding this matters for evangelism: no one is without some sense of moral accountability to answer for, which is precisely what makes the Gospel’s offer of Christ’s righteousness good news, not merely additional information.


### Key Discovery Questions

1. Romans 2:14-15 says even those without the written Law have "the work of the law written in their hearts." How have you seen conscience function this way, even in people who don’t profess faith?

2. James 2:10 says stumbling in one point makes a person "guilty of all." Why does this matter for anyone trying to be "good enough" for God?

3. What is the difference between the Law’s diagnostic function ("knowledge of sin," Romans 3:20) and a curative one?

4. Romans 10:4 says "Christ is the end of the law for righteousness." What does that mean for how you pursue righteousness now?

### Group Discussion Questions

1. Why do you think Jesus deliberately raised the Law’s standard to the level of the heart (Matthew 5:21-22, 27-28) rather than lowering it?

2. Share a time you tried to earn God’s approval through your own effort. What happened?

3. How does understanding the Law as a "tutor" (Galatians 3:24) rather than a ladder change how you read the Old Testament?

4. What is the risk of a Christian who has received grace but still, unconsciously, tries to maintain it through law-keeping?

### Application

The Law’s job was always to reveal, not to rescue. Let it do that work this week, then bring what it reveals to Christ.

- **Personal life.** Watch for any moment you catch yourself trying to "earn" a clear conscience through performance rather than resting in Christ’s righteousness.

- **Church community.** Read one of the Ten Commandments (Exodus 20:3-17) and let it show you, honestly, where you fall short, then bring that immediately to Christ, not to self-improvement.

- **Difficult seasons.** When guilt resurfaces over old failure, meditate on 2 Corinthians 5:21 as your identity, not your effort.

- **Scripture to carry.** Memorise Romans 3:20: "By the deeds of the law no flesh will be justified in His sight, for by the law is the knowledge of sin." Let it dismantle any remaining reliance on self-effort for right standing with God.

### Revision Cards

- **Q:** What is the Law’s actual function, according to Romans 3:20?  
  **A:** "By the deeds of the law no flesh will be justified in His sight, for by the law is the knowledge of sin." Romans 3:20

- **Q:** What happens if someone keeps the whole Law but stumbles in one point? (James 2:10)  
  **A:** "For whoever shall keep the whole law, and yet stumble in one point, he is guilty of all." James 2:10

- **Q:** What did Jesus say about murder and the heart? (Matthew 5:21-22)  
  **A:** Jesus taught that anger in the heart carries the same root guilt as the act of murder, tracing the Law’s standard to the heart, not just outward action.

- **Q:** What role did the Law play, according to Galatians? (Galatians 3:24)  
  **A:** "The law was our tutor to bring us to Christ, that we might be justified by faith." Galatians 3:24

  **A:** "For Christ is the end of the law for righteousness to everyone who believes." Romans 10:4

# Lesson 09: Not Under Law, but Under Grace
*Lesson 9 of 16  |  Section C: Living Free from Law and Sin*

### Opening Question
**When you fail, is your instinct to try harder to make up for it, or to run straight to God? What does that instinct reveal about what you actually believe about grace?**

### Key Scripture
**Romans 6:14**
> *For sin shall not have dominion over you, for you are not under law but under grace.*

*Read this slowly before moving on. Notice what breaks sin’s dominion here: grace, not effort.*

### Core Truth
**Grace is not merely forgiveness for past failure; it is God’s unearned, undeserved favour and ability, freely given, that replaces performance as the basis of standing with Him.**

### Bible Teaching

#### Favour plus ability

Grace is defined plainly as the unearned, undeserved favour and ability of God, not a reward for effort, but a gift that precedes and enables it.

**Ephesians 2:8-9**
> *For by grace you have been saved through faith, and that not of yourselves; it is the gift of God, not of works, lest anyone should boast.*

#### Impossible to earn

Attempting to earn divine favour through flawless performance is, in this lesson’s own image, like trying to defy gravity: it cannot be sustained no matter how sincere the effort.

**Titus 3:5**
> *Not by works of righteousness which we have done, but according to His mercy He saved us, through the washing of regeneration and renewing of the Holy Spirit.*

#### Christ’s sufficiency, not a discount

Christ’s sacrifice did not merely reduce the penalty for sin; it removed it entirely, providing His own righteousness as a gift.

**Romans 5:17**
> *For if by the one man’s offense death reigned through the one, much more those who receive abundance of grace and of the gift of righteousness will reign in life through the One, Jesus Christ.*

#### Guarding against self-focus

A believer’s focus on personal failure, rather than on Christ’s finished, redemptive work, quietly weakens faith rather than strengthening holiness.

**Proverbs 4:23**
> *Keep your heart with all diligence, for out of it spring the issues of life.*

**Caution:** Being "not under law but under grace" does not grant license to sin freely. Grace removes sin’s dominion by changing the basis of the believer’s identity and empowerment; it does not excuse indulging in what it has freed you from.

3. How do you answer someone who says grace is "too easy" or gives people permission to sin?

4. What practical difference does Romans 5:17 make, that believers are "made righteous," not merely forgiven?

### Application

Grace is not only pardon for what is past; it is ability for what is ahead. Lean on it deliberately this week.

- **Personal life.** Notice any moment you try to "earn" a sense of God’s favour through effort, and consciously return to grace instead.

- **Church community.** Practise Proverbs 4:23 deliberately: when a failure comes to mind, redirect your focus to Christ’s finished work rather than dwelling on the failure itself.

- **Difficult seasons.** When guilt threatens to define a hard season, study Romans 5:17 and 6:14 together and let them reshape your understanding of grace as favour plus ability, not just pardon.

- **Scripture to carry.** Memorise Romans 6:14: "For sin shall not have dominion over you, for you are not under law but under grace." Let it be your answer the next time guilt tries to reassert control.

# Lesson 10: No More Consciousness of Sin
*Lesson 10 of 16  |  Section C: Living Free from Law and Sin*

### Opening Question
**Do you ever feel like you have to keep "paying" for past sin, even though you know it’s forgiven? Where does that feeling come from?**

### Key Scripture
**Hebrews 10:14**
> *For by one offering He has perfected forever those who are being sanctified.*

*Read this slowly before moving on. Notice both words held together: "perfected" and "forever."*

### Core Truth
**Christ’s single sacrifice was sufficient to deal with sin permanently; believers are no longer meant to carry an ongoing consciousness of guilt.**

### Bible Teaching

#### The old system’s built-in limitation

Old Testament sacrifices had to be repeated endlessly, and that very repetition proved they could never fully deal with sin, leaving worshippers perpetually conscious of it.

**Hebrews 10:1**
> *For the law, having a shadow of the good things to come, and not the very image of the things, can never with these same sacrifices, which they offer continually year by year, make those who approach perfect.*

#### One offering, complete

Christ’s offering, by contrast, was made once and perfected forever those who are being sanctified: a completed, sufficient work.

**Hebrews 9:12**
> *Not with the blood of goats and calves, but with His own blood He entered the Most Holy Place once for all, having obtained eternal redemption.*

#### God’s deliberate non-remembrance

Under the New Covenant, God does not merely tolerate forgiven sin; He declares that He will remember it no more.

**Hebrews 8:12**
> *For I will be merciful to their unrighteousness, and their sins and their lawless deeds I will remember no more.*

Salvation operates entirely through God’s undeserved favour, not through ongoing religious duty or performance meant to maintain standing.

**Romans 8:1**
> *There is therefore now no condemnation to those who are in Christ Jesus, who do not walk according to the flesh, but according to the Spirit.*

**Going Deeper:** A believer who cannot let go of forgiven sin is, functionally, still living by the old covenant’s logic of repeated, never-quite-sufficient payment, rather than resting in the New Covenant’s one, complete sacrifice. Persistent, unresolved guilt after genuine repentance is not evidence of humility; it denies the sufficiency of the cross.

**God’s love for the believer is already complete and cannot be increased by good behaviour or decreased by failure; righteousness in Christ is a permanent gift, not a fluctuating balance.**

#### No ceiling on God’s love

God’s love is not on a sliding scale. His love was already fully expressed at the cross; better behaviour does not earn more of it.

# Lesson 12: The Fruit of Salvation (Part 1)
*Lesson 12 of 16  |  Section D: Living as a Disciple*

### Opening Question
**If someone watched your life closely for a month, without hearing you say a single word about your faith, what would they conclude you believe?**

### Key Scripture
**James 2:18**
> *But someone will say, "You have faith, and I have works." Show me your faith without your works, and I will show you my faith by my works.*

*Read this slowly before moving on. James is not arguing against faith; he is asking what genuine faith looks like.*

### Core Truth
**Genuine saving faith is not a one-time decision that ends; it is a continuous, living trust that naturally produces visible fruit over time.**

### Bible Teaching

#### A continuing trust, not a closed decision

Many New Testament commands use a verb form indicating action that is repeated, continuous, and habitual, a lifestyle, not a single completed event. Genuine faith persists and deepens; it does not simply conclude.

**Galatians 2:20**
> *I have been crucified with Christ; it is no longer I who live, but Christ lives in me; and the life which I now live in the flesh I live by faith in the Son of God, who loved me and gave Himself for me.*

#### Fruit as evidence, not payment

Fruit is evidence of genuine salvation, never the means of earning it. What we do is the outcome of what we believe, not the cause of God’s acceptance.

**Matthew 7:16-17**
> *You will know them by their fruits. Do men gather grapes from thornbushes or figs from thistles? Even so, every good tree bears good fruit, but a bad tree bears bad fruit.*

#### Works of the Law, and works of faith

Scripture distinguishes "works of the Law," attempts to earn salvation, from "works of faith," the fruit that flows out of salvation already received.

**Galatians 2:16**
> *Knowing that a man is not justified by the works of the law but by faith in Jesus Christ, even we have believed in Christ Jesus, that we might be justified by faith in Christ and not by the works of the law.*

#### Faith without works is dead

**James 2:17**
> *Thus also faith by itself, if it does not have works, is dead.*

**Note:** James is not contradicting salvation by grace through faith; he is describing what living faith actually looks like in practice. Faith that produces nothing is not a lesser but valid kind of faith; James calls it dead.

**Going Deeper:** A Greek-grammar observation is worth holding carefully: commands like "believe" often appear in a present-tense form in the original text, meaning "keep on believing" rather than "believe once." This does not add a work to salvation; it clarifies that genuine faith, by its nature, does not stop.

# Lesson 14: A Call to Discipleship
*Lesson 14 of 16  |  Section D: Living as a Disciple*

### Opening Question
**Has anyone ever personally invested in helping you grow as a follower of Christ, one-on-one, over time? What difference did that make?**

### Key Scripture
**Luke 14:33**
> *So likewise, whoever of you does not forsake all that he has cannot be My disciple.*

*Read this slowly before moving on. Jesus does not soften this cost to make it easier to hear.*

### Core Truth
**Christ’s commission was never merely to make converts; it was to make disciples, transformed believers capable of reproducing genuine faith in others.**

### Bible Teaching

#### A cost named plainly

Jesus deliberately distinguished discipleship from casual interest, calling for a level of commitment that would "forsake all," not a one-time decision alone.

**Luke 14:26**
> *If anyone comes to Me and does not hate his father and mother, wife and children, brothers and sisters, yes, and his own life also, he cannot be My disciple.*

#### Make disciples, not only converts

The Great Commission’s actual instruction is to make disciples, not simply to secure decisions or count converts.

**Matthew 28:19-20**
> *Go therefore and make disciples of all the nations, baptizing them in the name of the Father and of the Son and of the Holy Spirit, teaching them to observe all things that I have commanded you.*

#### Multiplication, not only addition

A discipleship approach that reproduces itself multiplies exponentially over time, in principle reaching far more people over years than large-scale but shallow campaigns.

**Going Deeper:** A mathematical illustration is worth working through slowly: one person discipling another, who then disciples another, doubling consistently. Over roughly twelve to fifteen years of consistent doubling, the numbers surpass what mass crusades typically reach in the same period, not because reproduction is faster in any single instance, but because it compounds.

#### Christian and disciple: one commitment

"Christian" and "disciple" are meant to describe the same level of commitment; the terms were used interchangeably in the early church.

**Acts 11:26**
> *And when he had found him, he brought him to Antioch. So it was that for a whole year they assembled with the church and taught a great many people. And the disciples were first called Christians in Antioch.*

**Note:** Discipleship requires patient, personal investment, much like raising a child, not a single event but sustained responsibility over time.

### Application

Discipleship is personal and sustained, not a single event. Take one real step toward investing in someone this week.

# Lesson 15: How to Use Your Testimony
*Lesson 15 of 16  |  Section D: Living as a Disciple*

### Opening Question
**If a stranger asked you, right now, "how did you become a Christian?", could you answer clearly in under two minutes?**

### Key Scripture
**Acts 26:16**
> *But rise and stand on your feet; for I have appeared to you for this purpose, to make you a minister and a witness both of the things which you have seen and of the things which I will yet reveal to you.*

*Read this slowly before moving on. Notice the role given here: witness, someone reporting what was seen.*

### Core Truth
**A personal testimony, told simply and honestly, is one of the most effective and accessible evangelism tools every believer already has.**

### Bible Teaching

#### A story people can connect to

Personal testimony makes the Gospel relatable in a way abstract argument often cannot; people connect to a real story before they connect to a doctrine.

**Acts 4:20**
> *For we cannot but speak the things which we have seen and heard.*

#### Paul’s own repeated pattern

The apostle Paul modelled testimony-sharing directly, recounting his own conversion story multiple times in Acts: the event itself in Acts 9, and two further retellings before different audiences in Acts 22 and Acts 26.

#### A low-pressure approach

Visiting homes to offer prayer, then sharing a personal conversion story, is presented as an approachable, low-pressure way to begin gospel conversations.

**Acts 16:14**
> *Now a certain woman named Lydia heard us. She was a seller of purple from the city of Thyatira, who worshiped God. The Lord opened her heart to heed the things spoken by Paul.*

#### Every genuine story counts

Even believers without a dramatic conversion story can testify effectively; the substance of a testimony is the reality of transformation, not the drama of the before-and-after.

**Going Deeper:** A believer raised in the faith from childhood, with no single crisis moment to point to, still has a genuine testimony of God’s ongoing work, and that story carries equal weight to a more dramatic one.

#### Relationship over pressure

Effective evangelism prioritises relationship-building and genuine dialogue over rushing someone toward an immediate decision in a single encounter.

**Romans 10:14-15**
> *How then shall they call on Him in whom they have not believed? And how shall they believe in Him of whom they have not heard? And how shall they hear without a preacher?*

**Caution:** Pressuring an immediate decision can feel manipulative or rushed, and it can close a door that patient, relational conversation would have kept open.


### Key Discovery Questions

1. Could you currently share your own testimony clearly in under two minutes? What would you need to clarify or shorten?

2. Why do you think Paul relied on retelling his personal testimony (Acts 9, 22, 26) rather than only theological argument?

3. What is the difference between "prayer-walking" style evangelism and pressuring an immediate decision, and why does the lesson favour the former?

4. If you don’t have a dramatic conversion story, what genuine, ongoing work of God in your life could still form the substance of a testimony?

### Group Discussion Questions

1. Why might a personal testimony reach someone that a theological argument alone would not?

2. Share (briefly) your own testimony with the group, as practice for sharing it outside this setting.

3. What makes pressuring an immediate decision less effective, or even counterproductive, compared to relational evangelism?

4. How can quieter, less dramatic testimonies be encouraged and valued as much as more dramatic ones in a church community?

### Application

Your story is a tool you already have. Prepare it and use it this week.

- **Personal life.** Draft a two-minute version of your own testimony this week: before, turning point, after.

- **Church community.** Share your testimony with one trusted person this week, purely for practice.

- **Difficult seasons.** Look for one low-pressure opportunity this week, a conversation, a prayer offered, to open a door rather than push for an immediate decision.

- **Scripture to carry.** Memorise Acts 26:16: "I have appeared to you for this purpose, to make you a minister and a witness both of the things which you have seen." Let it settle your role as witness, not persuader.
