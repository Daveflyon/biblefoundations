Teacher’s Guide

Bible Truth Foundations – Part 2 of 3: Growing in Christ

The Well Shrewsbury

Part A: How to Use This Guide

This guide accompanies the sixteen student lessons of Part 2 and is for facilitators only; it is never distributed with the student files. Each lesson entry below gives the facilitator’s aim, the key scripture and core truth as they appear in the student material, specific things to watch for in the room, and a pastoral note. The student lesson carries the full teaching text; your role is not to re-teach it but to steward the discussion it opens.

Suggested session shape (75–90 minutes): open with the Opening Question cold, before any teaching (10 minutes); read the Key Scripture aloud and let the group sit with it (5); walk the Bible Teaching section together, expanding where the room needs it (25–30); Group Discussion Questions (20); Application and prayer (15). Key Discovery Questions are completed by members at home before the session; begin discussion by asking what they found.

All scripture in the series is New King James Version, declared once at series level. British English throughout. Lessons build in order; resist requests to jump ahead, especially to Lessons 7–9, which depend on Lessons 5–6.

Part B: Per-Lesson Guide

Section A — Mind and Word

Lesson 01: Self-Centredness

Facilitator’s aim: Expose self-centredness as the single root of strife and point to the crucified life as its cure.

Key Scripture: Proverbs 13:10 — By pride comes nothing but strife, But with the well-advised is wisdom.

Core Truth

Strife has only one root, and it is not what happens to you; it is self sitting at the centre of your life. Peace begins where self is dethroned and Christ is Lord.

Watch for:

  • The low self-esteem point lands hard; give it time. Someone will push back that humility means thinking little of yourself; let Numbers 12 answer.
  • Keep the session from becoming a clinic on other people's pride. Steer every example back to the person speaking.
  • The Opening Question can surface live conflicts. Acknowledge, park gently, return privately afterwards.

Pastoral note

Expect at least one member to realise mid-session that a current conflict is theirs to end. Do not force public confession; a quiet word after is worth more.

Lesson 02: How to Meditate on God's Word

Facilitator’s aim: Equip every member with at least one usable method of meditation and lift the aim from technique to communion.

Key Scripture: Joshua 1:8 — This Book of the Law shall not depart from your mouth, but you shall meditate in it day and night, that you may observe to do according to all that is written in it. For then you will make your way prosperous, and then you will have good success.

Core Truth

Meditation is not emptying your mind; it is filling it with God's Word and turning that Word over until you meet the God behind it.

Watch for:

  • Distinguish biblical meditation from Eastern emptying early; the confusion is common and sincere.
  • The four methods can overwhelm beginners. Have each person pick one, not master four.
  • Watch for guilt about past Bible-reading failure; the lesson is an invitation, not an audit.

Pastoral note

Set the group a shared one-verse practice for the week and open the next session by asking how it went. Accountability turns this lesson from theory into habit.

Lesson 03: Renewing the Mind

Facilitator’s aim: Establish that thoughts are chosen dwellings and train the replacement principle as a daily discipline.

Key Scripture: Romans 12:2 — And do not be conformed to this world, but be transformed by the renewing of your mind, that you may prove what is that good and acceptable and perfect will of God.

Core Truth

You cannot always choose the thoughts that arrive, but you always choose the thoughts that stay. Transformation happens where thoughts are dwelt on.

Watch for:

  • The pink elephant illustration is worth doing live; suppression versus replacement must be felt, not just heard.
  • Someone may hear 'choose your thoughts' as dismissing real mental health struggles. Be clear this is discipleship, not a substitute for professional care.
  • Romans 12:1 precedes 12:2; do not let the group skip the presented body on the way to the renewed mind.

Pastoral note

This lesson pairs naturally with Lesson 2; refer back constantly. Meditation is the mechanism by which renewal actually happens.

Section B — Church and Authority

Lesson 04: The Importance of Christ's Church

Facilitator’s aim: Recover the biblical meaning of church as gathered people and move members from attendance to participation.

Key Scripture: Hebrews 10:24–25 — And let us consider one another in order to stir up love and good works, not forsaking the assembling of ourselves together, as is the manner of some, but exhorting one another, and so much the more as you see the Day approaching.

Core Truth

The church is not a building you attend but a people you belong to; wherever believers gather in Jesus' name to build one another up, the church is meeting.

Watch for:

  • This lesson can be heard as an attack on Sunday services or on church buildings; it is neither. Hold both-and firmly: the point is what church is, not where.
  • The 1 Corinthians 14:26 picture may be unfamiliar; let its strangeness do its work before explaining it away.
  • Watch for the hurt member using this lesson to justify isolation; Hebrews 10:25 cuts the other way.

Pastoral note

End by asking each member what they bring to the gathering. The lesson has failed if everyone leaves still thinking of church as something attended.

Lesson 05: Deliverance

Facilitator’s aim: Remove fear of the demonic by establishing Christ's victory, the believer's position and the Word as the weapon.

Key Scripture: 1 John 3:8 — He who sins is of the devil, for the devil has sinned from the beginning. For this purpose the Son of God was manifested, that He might destroy the works of the devil.

Core Truth

Jesus came to destroy the works of the devil, and He has armed His people with His Word and His name; the believer fights from victory, not for it.

Watch for:

  • Tone is everything: sober, unafraid, undramatic. Neither jokes nor thrills serve this subject.
  • Some members carry real experiences they have never voiced in church. Make room without letting testimony swamp teaching.
  • James 4:7's order, submit then resist, protects the group from bravado. Stress it.

Pastoral note

If a member discloses ongoing oppression, arrange prayer with mature believers outside the session rather than turning the group meeting into a deliverance service.

Lesson 06: Authority of the Believer

Facilitator’s aim: Trace the Bible's authority story from Eden to the Great Commission so believers pray and stand from victory.

Key Scripture: Matthew 28:18 — And Jesus came and spoke to them, saying, "All authority has been given to Me in heaven and on earth."

Core Truth

Satan's only real weapon is deception; Christ holds all authority and shares it with His people, so the believer who knows the truth cannot be ruled by a defeated enemy.

Watch for:

  • The Genesis narrative carries the lesson; walk it slowly. If the group grasps that Satan's only tool is deception, everything else follows.
  • Guard against triumphalism: shared authority is exercised under Christ, in His name, mostly by refusing lies dwelling space.
  • 2 Corinthians 10:5 makes this practical; without it the lesson stays theoretical.

Pastoral note

Link forward: authority (this lesson) underlies healing (Lessons 7 and 8) and answered prayer (Lesson 16). Plant those flags now.

Section C — Healing and Relationships

Lesson 07: Healing is in the Atonement

Facilitator’s aim: Establish from Matthew 8:17 and 1 Peter 2:24 that physical healing was purchased at the cross.

Key Scripture: Isaiah 53:4–5 — Surely He has borne our griefs And carried our sorrows; Yet we esteemed Him stricken, Smitten by God, and afflicted. But He was wounded for our transgressions, He was bruised for our iniquities; The chastisement for our peace was upon Him, And by His stripes we are healed.

Core Truth

At the cross Jesus carried sins and sicknesses together; healing is not a lucky extra but a purchased provision of the atonement.

Watch for:

  • Someone in the room is sick or loves someone who is. Teach provision boldly and pastor gently; the lesson text models the balance in its final section.
  • The sozo word study convinces heads; the healing narratives convince hearts. Use both.
  • Do not let debate about why some are not healed consume the session; that is Lesson 8's territory. Park it explicitly.

Pastoral note

Close by actually praying for the sick present, on the basis taught. A lesson on healing that ends without prayer for healing has contradicted itself.

Lesson 08: Hindrances to Healing

Facilitator’s aim: Name the real hindrances, ignorance, sin's inroads, unbelief, while keeping all condemnation off the sick.

Key Scripture: Hosea 4:6 — My people are destroyed for lack of knowledge. Because you have rejected knowledge, I also will reject you from being priest for Me; Because you have forgotten the law of your God, I also will forget your children.

Core Truth

God's willingness to heal is settled at the cross; where healing is hindered, the blockage lies in ignorance, sin's inroads or unbelief, never in God's reluctance.

Watch for:

  • This is the most pastorally delicate lesson in Part 2. The caution against morbid introspection is not an aside; it is half the lesson.
  • Never let the group diagnose absent third parties, and never a present member without their invitation.
  • The Nazareth passage raises the atmosphere question; discuss corporate faith without assigning blame for past disappointments.

Pastoral note

If grief surfaces over someone prayed for and lost, stop and minister. The teaching will keep; the person will not.

Lesson 09: Forgiving Others

Facilitator’s aim: Move every member from counting offences to cancelling debts, with Mark 11:25 as the standing deadline.

Key Scripture: Matthew 18:21–22 — Then Peter came to Him and said, "Lord, how often shall my brother sin against me, and I forgive him? Up to seven times?" Jesus said to him, "I do not say to you, up to seven times, but up to seventy times seven."

Core Truth

Forgiven people forgive; the debt others owe you is pocket change beside the debt God cancelled for you, and refusing to release it hands you to the tormentors.

Watch for:

  • The Opening Question does the work; do not rush it. Names will surface.
  • Be precise on what forgiveness is not: excusing, trusting again, reconciling. Muddling these keeps people stuck.
  • Severe wounds, abuse among them, need the 'instalments' pastoral note and likely private follow-up. Say so aloud.

Pastoral note

Allow silence at the end for private release before God. Consider closing with each member praying Mark 11:25 quietly over one name.

Lesson 10: Marriage (Part 1)

Facilitator’s aim: Establish marriage as God's good design and love as learned, Christ-centred action.

Key Scripture: Genesis 2:24 — Therefore a man shall leave his father and mother and be joined to his wife, and they shall become one flesh.

Core Truth

Marriage is God's good design, a man, a woman and God joined in one purpose, and it thrives not on found feelings but on learned, Christ-centred love.

Watch for:

  • Mixed groups need care: singles, the widowed and the divorced are all present in most rooms. The design is taught without turning marriage into a status hierarchy.
  • The 'love is learned' point liberates those from broken homes; draw it out.
  • Keep the session off the rails of gender-role debate; the lesson's centre is Genesis 2:24 and Christ at the centre.

Pastoral note

Encourage couples to do the Matthew 7:12 experiment together and report back. Marriages move on practice, not on notes taken.

Lesson 11: Marriage (Part 2)

Facilitator’s aim: Deepen Part 1's design into covenant: binding oath, life-giving words, Christ-and-church mystery.

Key Scripture: Ephesians 5:25 — Husbands, love your wives, just as Christ also loved the church and gave Himself for her.

Core Truth

Marriage is a covenant, not a contract: a binding oath of love kept by daily choices and life-giving words, mirroring Christ and His church.

Watch for:

  • Contract versus covenant is the hinge; land it with examples until the difference is felt.
  • The words section convicts quickly. Watch for spouses glancing at each other; keep the room safe and the application personal, not marital point-scoring.
  • Public teasing of spouses may be this group's culture; address it from Ezekiel 16:8's covering without shaming anyone.

Pastoral note

The thirty-day word of life practice is the take-home. Set it as a group commitment for couples and a prayer commitment for others.

Lesson 12: God's Kind of Love (Part 1)

Facilitator’s aim: Distinguish the four loves and establish agape as chosen commitment that generates feeling.

Key Scripture: 1 Corinthians 13:13 — And now abide faith, hope, love, these three; but the greatest of these is love.

Core Truth

God's kind of love, agape, is not a feeling that comes and goes but a chosen commitment to another's good, and it is the one thing you keep beyond the grave.

Watch for:

  • The Greek framework organises the whole lesson; teach the four words until the group can use them.
  • 'Acting without feeling is hypocrisy' will be raised. The enemy-love passages settle it: obedience first, feelings follow.
  • The refugee-family story illustrates agape generating phileo; retell it or substitute a local equivalent.

Pastoral note

Assign the three-acts experiment and open next session with results. Agape taught without assignment evaporates.

Lesson 13: God's Kind of Love (Part 2)

Facilitator’s aim: Press love from definition to deed: nonverbal, aimed at the least, received by the King.

Key Scripture: Matthew 25:40 — And the King will answer and say to them, "Assuredly, I say to you, inasmuch as you did it to one of the least of these My brethren, you did it to Me."

Core Truth

Love is something you do: nearly all of it is nonverbal, the least of these are its address, and Jesus Himself receives every deed of it.

Watch for:

  • Matthew 25 must not be softened into sentiment; its deeds are concrete and checkable.
  • Handle the works question head-on if raised: deeds evidence love, they do not purchase salvation.
  • The 'second recipient' truth, serving Jesus in the least, reframes every act of service; give it full weight.

Pastoral note

Best closed with planning, not discussion: agree one concrete Matthew 25 act the group will do together this month.

Section D — Provision and Prayer

Lesson 14: Finances (Part 1)

Facilitator’s aim: Correct both poverty-religion and greed-religion with the Bible's own definition of prosperity.

Key Scripture: 2 Corinthians 9:8 — And God is able to make all grace abound toward you, that you, always having all sufficiency in all things, may have an abundance for every good work.

Core Truth

God cares about your provision, spirit, soul and body, and biblical prosperity is having all sufficiency plus an abundance, not to hoard but for every good work.

Watch for:

  • Money talk raises defences; the two-ditch framing lowers them. Name both errors before anyone assigns you one.
  • 1 Timothy 6:10 is universally misquoted; fix it: the love of money, a root, not money, the root.
  • Keep testimony of provision welcome but brief; the lesson's spine is purpose: blessed to be a blessing.

Pastoral note

The generosity wish list exercise reveals hearts gently. Invite sharing but never require it; some lists are between a member and God.

Lesson 15: Finances (Part 2)

Facilitator’s aim: Establish giving as the engine of biblical finance: proportional, cheerful, first.

Key Scripture: Luke 6:38 — Give, and it will be given to you: good measure, pressed down, shaken together, and running over will be put into your bosom. For with the same measure that you use, it will be measured back to you.

Core Truth

Giving is the engine of biblical finance: measured by proportion not amount, powered by love not obligation, and trusted to a God who repays what is lent to Him.

Watch for:

  • The widow's mites dismantle 'I cannot afford to give'; let the passage do it rather than pressing anyone.
  • Be scrupulously clean here: no fundraising, no amounts, no comparisons. Any whiff of manipulation poisons the lesson.
  • Giving-to-get deserves direct treatment; 2 Corinthians 9:7 is the filter.

Pastoral note

If your church has been hurt by money-preaching, acknowledge it plainly at the start. Honesty buys the lesson its hearing.

Lesson 16: When Prayers Seem Unanswered

Facilitator’s aim: Send the group out confident that prayers according to God's will are heard, and equipped for the gap before manifestation.

Key Scripture: 1 John 5:14–15 — Now this is the confidence that we have in Him, that if we ask anything according to His will, He hears us. And if we know that He hears us, whatever we ask, we know that we have the petitions that we have asked of Him.

Core Truth

God answers prayer offered in faith according to His Word; when answers tarry, the delay is in the delivery, never in His willingness to hear.

Watch for:

  • Daniel's twenty-one days is the load-bearing passage; walk it verse by verse.
  • Almost everyone carries a long-unanswered prayer. The three checks (Word, motive, relationships) must be offered as tools, never as verdicts on their waiting.
  • End the course looking forward: thanksgiving in the gap is the posture that carries everything from Part 2 into daily life.

Pastoral note

Close the whole of Part 2 by praying together over the group's longest-standing requests, with thanksgiving. Start the answer-book that night.
