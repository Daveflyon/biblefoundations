Part 3 of 3: Serving in Christ

The Power of Godly Relationships

Lesson 4 of 16  |  Section B: Living Among Others

Opening Question

Please begin by answering this question honestly in your own words.

Think about the people you spend the most time with. Are they, on balance, pulling you closer to God or quietly pulling you away? How would you actually know?

Key Scripture

1 Corinthians 15:33

Do not be deceived: "Evil company corrupts good habits."

Read this slowly before moving on. Deception is the word Paul uses; this is not a truth we naturally believe without being told.

Core Truth

The central idea of this lesson

Who you surround yourself with shapes who you become; godly relationships are not optional extras to the Christian life, they are how God grows and protects it.

Called out together, not called out alone

The New Testament word for church, ekklesia, means "a called out group." Christianity was never designed to be lived in isolation from other believers.

1 Corinthians 12:12

For as the body is one and has many members, but all the members of that one body, being many, are one body, so also is Christ.

The body of Christ functions like a physical body: each member has a distinct gift and role, and the body suffers when members disconnect from one another.

Confession and accountability

Confession of struggles to trustworthy, godly people produces healing and accountability. This is different from confession of sin for forgiveness, which belongs to God alone.

James 5:16

Confess your trespasses to one another, and pray for one another, that you may be healed. The effective, fervent prayer of a righteous man avails much.

Going Deeper

Confessing sin to God alone secures forgiveness (1 John 1:9), but confessing struggles to trusted, godly people (James 5:16) secures something different: ongoing accountability and healing that a private relationship with God, on its own, was never designed to replace.

Exhortation and the danger of drifting

Scripture commands believers to exhort one another daily and warns against forsaking the assembling of believers, because neglecting it leaves the heart open to being hardened through the deceitfulness of sin.

Hebrews 10:25

Not forsaking the assembling of ourselves together, as is the manner of some, but exhorting one another, and so much the more as you see the Day approaching.

Company shapes character

Ungodly company is not neutral; it actively corrupts good habits and thinking. This is why Scripture repeatedly warns against close association with those who will pull a believer away from God.

Proverbs 13:20

He who walks with wise men will be wise, but the companion of fools will be destroyed.

Caution

This is not a call to abandon relationships with those who do not yet know Christ; loving people far from God is part of the Christian life. It is a warning about close, formative company that consistently pulls you toward compromise rather than growth.

Question My Answer Group Discussion Notes
Who are the two or three people who most exhort or challenge you spiritually? When did you last let them into a real struggle?
What is the difference between confessing sin to God for forgiveness and confessing struggles to another believer for accountability (James 5:16)?
Proverbs 13:20 says "he who walks with wise men will be wise." Look honestly at who you walk with most; what is it shaping in you?
What is one step you could take this week to be less isolated in your walk with Christ?

These questions are designed for open conversation at any level of experience. There are no trick questions and no single correct answer.

  1. Why do you think the earliest church is described as devoting itself to "fellowship" as its own distinct practice, alongside teaching and prayer (Acts 2:42)?
  2. Share a time a godly relationship changed the direction of a struggle you were facing.
  3. What makes it hard to confess a real struggle to another believer, even one we trust?
  4. How do you discern the line between loving people who are far from God and letting "evil company" corrupt your own habits (1 Corinthians 15:33)?

Godly relationships are not automatic; they take deliberate honesty and presence. Take one real step this week.

Context How I Apply This
Personal life List the three to five people you spend the most time with. Honestly assess whether that company is drawing you toward or away from God.
Church community Share one real struggle with a trustworthy, godly person this week rather than carrying it alone.
Difficult seasons If you have been drifting from corporate gathering, commit to being present this week specifically, not out of obligation but as protection for your own heart (Hebrews 10:25).
Scripture to carry Memorise 1 Corinthians 15:33: "Do not be deceived: ‘Evil company corrupts good habits.’" Let it prompt an honest look at your closest relationships.
